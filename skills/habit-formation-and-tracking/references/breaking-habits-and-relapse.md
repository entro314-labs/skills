# Breaking Habits and Relapse Handling

## Reverse framework

1. **Identify the cue**: time, place, emotional state, preceding action, people present.
2. **Identify the underlying reward**, not the surface behavior. Scrolling = stimulation plus escape from boredom; evening snacking = comfort or transition.
3. **Increase friction**: delete the app, move the phone out of the room, set limits, do not stock the item.
4. **Remove or change the cue**: change location or routine, or fill the slot immediately after the trigger with something else.
5. **Replace with a routine that delivers the same reward.**
6. **Write an implementation intention for the urge**: "When I feel the urge to [X], I will [replacement] instead."

## Replacement table

| Underlying need | Replacement options |
|---|---|
| Boredom | puzzle, book, short walk |
| Stress | movement, cold water, slow breathing |
| Comfort eating | bath, weighted blanket, call a friend |
| Transition after work | porch sit, shower, change of clothes |
| Social stimulation | message a friend, brief call |

## Which lever for which habit

| Type | Strongest lever |
|---|---|
| Behavioral (scrolling, gambling, overeating) | Environment design and friction |
| Substance (nicotine, alcohol, drugs) | Behavioral techniques as a complement to medical support, never a replacement |

## Cravings

- Cravings typically peak and pass within about 10-15 minutes (rule of thumb, not a precise finding). Delay and substitute during the peak.
- Each urge ridden out without giving in tends to make the next one smaller.
- The first 2-4 weeks are usually the hardest (rule of thumb).
- Plan the replacement before the urge arrives; decisions during a craving are weak.

## Relapse handling

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| One slip | Treat as data, not failure. Log it, identify the cue, adjust the plan, resume at once. |
| Second consecutive slip | Intervene immediately with the tiny version or the replacement routine; this is the start of a new pattern. |
| Pattern on specific days/places | Breakdown-point analysis: write a specific backup per trigger. |
| Habit keeps failing | The plan is too big or the cue is wrong. Shrink, re-anchor. |
| Self-criticism spiral ("I'm not someone who does this") | Reframe by identity votes: one miss does not erase the majority; act on the next cue. |

Rules: never double up to compensate for a miss; do not reset counters punitively; judge the pattern, not one day.

## Addiction and safety boundary

- If use is compulsive and the person cannot control it despite negative consequences, recommend professional help. Habit techniques are supplementary.
- Withdrawal from alcohol or some drugs can be medically dangerous; direct the person to a clinician before they stop abruptly.
- Point to local health services or a national helpline for the person's country. Do not quote phone numbers from memory.
- Coaching copy must stay supportive and non-judgmental; no moralizing.
