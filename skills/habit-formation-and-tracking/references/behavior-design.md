# Behavior Design: Technique Catalog

## 1. Habit loop

| Part | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Cue | What triggers it? | time of day, location, emotional state (bored, stressed, tired), preceding action, other people |
| Routine | What is the behavior? | 2 pushups, one page, a walk |
| Reward | What does it give? | relief, stimulation, transition, a visible check mark |

Build: design all three. Change: keep cue and reward, swap the routine.
Worked swap (after-work beer): cue = walking in the door, reward = work-to-home transition, new routine = sparkling water plus a 5-minute porch sit.

## 2. Diagnose by failing lever

| Symptom | Failing lever | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Never starts | Not obvious | Stronger cue: anchor, visible object, alarm tied to a place |
| Starts, does not repeat | Not attractive/satisfying | Immediate reward, visible tracking, pair with something enjoyed |
| Misses often | Not easy | Shrink; cut steps; move materials closer |
| Does it, then drops it after a few weeks | Not satisfying or wrong identity | Add feedback, reframe as identity, check for a breakdown point |

Change one lever at a time and observe for a week before changing another.

## 3. Shrinking (tiny habits, two-minute rule)

- Formula: "After I [existing habit], I will [tiny new behavior]."
- The tiny version should feel almost embarrassingly easy.
- Scale any habit to a version under 2 minutes: "read one page", "write one sentence", "put on running shoes".
- Floor/ceiling: the floor (tiny version) is mandatory even on the worst day; anything above is optional.
- If a habit keeps failing, it is too big. Shrink it again; do not lecture about motivation.
- Month one: the target is frequency (daily), not quality or duration.

| Goal | Tiny version | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise | 2 pushups | after brushing teeth |
| Write | 1 sentence | after pouring coffee |
| Calm | 3 deep breaths | after parking |
| Read | 1 page | after getting into bed |
| Tidy | load 1 dish | after finishing dinner |

## 4. Habit stacking

- The anchor must occur every day without fail, in the same place, at a time that fits the new behavior.
- Mismatches to avoid: kitchen habit on a bedroom anchor; meditation on a rushed morning.
- Anchor discovery exercise: write down everything done from waking to leaving the house, and from arriving home to going to bed. Each item is a candidate. Pick one, attach one tiny behavior.
- Example evening stack: finish dinner -> load one dish; brush teeth -> 2 pushups; get into bed -> read one page.
- Add stacks one at a time.

## 5. Environment design

Principle: wanted behavior easy and visible; unwanted behavior hard and invisible.

| Add (good habits) | Remove (bad habits) |
|---|---|
| Running clothes by the bed | Delete social apps from the phone (desktop only) |
| Fruit on the counter | Do not stock trigger foods |
| Book on the pillow | Unplug the TV after use |
| Water bottle filled at night, placed where seen first | Phone charges outside the bedroom |

20-second rule: if starting takes more than about 20 seconds of effort, it happens much less often. Bring wanted behaviors under that threshold; add at least 20 seconds of friction to unwanted ones. Each extra step is a barrier; friction, not willpower, is the lever. (Ego depletion as a mechanism has replication problems; the practical advice to not rely on willpower alone still holds.)

## 6. Identity

- Outcome framing: "I want to quit smoking", "I want to read more".
- Identity framing: "I'm not a smoker", "I'm a reader".
- Each action is a vote for the identity; a majority is enough, perfection is not needed.
- In-the-moment prompt: "What would a [identity] do right now?"
- Act as if, collect evidence, let identity follow. Appropriate for habits, not for emotions.

## 7. Implementation intentions

- Formula: "When [situation], then I will [behavior]" or "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]."
- Specificity test: it names a situation, a behavior, and ideally a time and place.
- Evidence: a meta-analysis found a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (about d = 0.65). Do not quote a "2x" or "2-3x" multiplier.
- Reverse form for urges: "When I feel the urge to [bad habit], I will [replacement] instead."
- Pair with a backup: "If [obstacle], then [alternative]."

## 8. Habit plan template

```
Identity:      I am someone who ______.
Tiny habit:    After I ______ (anchor), I will ______ (<2 min).
Floor:         On the worst day I will still ______.
Ceiling:       If I feel like it, I may ______.
Environment:   I will place ______ at ______ so the cue is visible.
               I will remove/hide ______ to add friction to ______.
Breakdown points and backups:
               When ______ (weekend/travel/stress), I will ______.
Tracking:      ______ (calendar X, checklist, simple log).
Rule:          Never miss twice.
Review:        Is the tiny version being done daily? If not, shrink it.
```

## 9. Start and scale

- Start moment: a life transition disrupts old cues and makes new ones easier to attach.
- Add the next habit only after the first feels automatic (about 30-60 days, or 2+ weeks of consistency).
- To scale up, raise the ceiling gradually (tiny -> small -> normal) while the floor stays unchanged.
