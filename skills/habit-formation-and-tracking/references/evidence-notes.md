# Evidence Notes: What to Cite and What to Avoid

| Claim | Status | How to state it |
|---|---|---|
| "21 days to form a habit" | Myth; traced to a 1960 observation about adjustment after plastic surgery | Do not state as fact. At most: "a popular but oversimplified threshold" |
| Median 66 days to automaticity | Supported (UCL study, 96 participants, 12 weeks; range 18-254 days) | "A study found a median of about 66 days, ranging from 18 to 254" |
| Simple habits form faster than complex | Supported by the same study | Drinking water faster than exercise |
| Study year | Online 2009, print 2010 | Write "2009/2010" |
| One missed day does not significantly change the trajectory | Supported by the same study | "A single missed day had little effect" |
| Simple 30 / moderate 60 / complex 90+ days | Heuristic, not a finding | Label as a rough planning range |
| Implementation intentions help | Supported; meta-analysis, medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (about d = 0.65) | Use effect-size framing; never "2x" or "2-3x" |
| Cravings pass in about 10-15 minutes | Unsourced rule of thumb | "Typically peak and pass within minutes to a quarter hour" |
| First 2-4 weeks hardest; 2-4 week plateau valley | Unsourced rule of thumb | Present as common experience |
| Telling no one about goals improves follow-through | Mixed evidence | Do not assert; optional |
| Willpower depletion as mechanism | Replication problems | Keep the advice (design environment, do not rely on willpower alone), drop the mechanism |
| Don't break the chain | Anecdotal technique | Name it as a technique, not a proven effect |
| Streak quality tiers, milestone list, confidence thresholds | Design choices | Do not present as research standards |
| Identity shift at 90 days, "who you are" at 365 | Heuristic framing | Use as motivational milestones only |

General rule: when unsure, say "roughly", "for many people", or "in one study", and prefer ranges over single numbers.
