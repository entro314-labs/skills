# Sleep and Burnout Recovery

Coaching scope: behavioral, non-medical. Sleep and burnout interact (poor sleep deepens exhaustion; exhaustion fragments sleep), so audit both, then sequence interventions. Start with sleep when the person is exhausted: it is the cheapest, fastest lever and makes every later burnout step easier.

## Guardrails (apply first, always)

- Behavioral only. Never recommend or adjust prescription sleep medication or supplements-as-treatment; refer to a clinician.
- Never say "just push through" (burnout) or "just relax" (sleep). Both have physiological drivers.
- Burnout is a response to sustained conditions, not a personal failing. Say so early.
- Assess burnout severity before giving a plan. Severe cases need professional care first.
- One step at a time. Give the next 1-3 actions, not the whole protocol.
- Set expectations: sleep changes take 7-14 days; burnout recovery takes weeks to months.
- Do not diagnose. Name patterns, flag red flags, refer.

## Escalate immediately (override the plan)

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Suicidal thoughts, self-harm, hopelessness | Treat as a medical situation, not a character flaw. US: call or text 988. Elsewhere: local emergency number or crisis line. Stop coaching content; encourage contact now. |
| Waking unrefreshed plus loud snoring, gasping, observed breathing pauses, or morning headaches | Possible sleep apnea. Recommend medical evaluation (a home sleep test is a common screening route). |
| Persistent low mood, loss of interest, or worsening after 4-6 weeks of active recovery | Burnout can coexist with clinical depression. See a primary-care clinician or therapist. Employee-assistance programs often give free initial sessions; sliding-scale community therapy exists for cost limits. |
| Severe burnout (see staging) | Recommend professional support; discuss medical leave or a major change as options. |
| No sleep improvement after 14 days of anchor + environment work | Sleep apnea screening, or referral to a CBT-I (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia) provider. CBT-I is guideline-recommended first-line for chronic insomnia and more durable than medication. |

## Step 1: Intake (both topics)

Ask briefly, one or two questions at a time.

Sleep audit (10 items): usual bedtime, usual wake time, time to fall asleep, number of night wakings, morning feel (1-5), alcohol as a sleep aid, screens in bed, last caffeine time, naps, bedroom dark/cool/quiet.

Interpretation:
- Falls asleep in under 5 minutes: possible chronic sleep deprivation.
- Unrefreshed regardless of duration: possible apnea, refer.
- Alcohol as sleep aid: address first.
- Last caffeine after 2pm: handle in the caffeine step.
- Racing thoughts at bedtime: anxiety is probably driving the sleep problem; use the brain dump and consider CBT-I, which targets bedtime rumination.

Burnout screen: three separate dimensions, not one number.
- Exhaustion, cynicism/detachment, reduced efficacy.
- Use a 1-5 frequency scale, 4 items per dimension; record the three subscores and the date as baseline.
- The subscale pattern is the signal. Total-score bands (12-24 mild, 25-40 moderate, 41-60 severe) are only a coaching heuristic; the validated inventory is a licensed instrument, so never present these bands as clinical results.
- Single threshold rule: treat the upper band (41+) or any depression/self-harm signal as "refer to a professional" (one threshold, not two).

Details and item wording guidance: `references/burnout-screen-and-drivers.md`.

## Step 2: Sleep protocol (ordered by impact)

1. Fixed wake time, 7 days a week (the anchor). Highest-priority lever. Get out of bed within ~10 minutes of the alarm; no lying in. No weekend catch-up: a ~90-minute weekend sleep-in resembles mild jet lag. Bedtime tends to drift earlier by itself within 1-2 weeks. Why: a consistent wake time gives a consistent build-up of sleep pressure each evening. Keep variation small (roughly 30 minutes or less is a practical target).
2. Stimulus control (core CBT-I rule). Bed is for sleep only. If not asleep after ~20 minutes, get up, do something dull in dim light, return only when sleepy. Do not clock-watch; turn clocks away.
3. Caffeine cutoff = bedtime minus 12 hours (half-life ~5-7 h; two half-lives leave ~25% at bedtime). Bedtime 10:30pm means last caffeine 10:30am. Longer for people over 50, on some medications, or slow metabolizers. Count hidden sources: tea, dark chocolate, "decaf" (small amounts), energy drinks and pre-workouts, some pain relievers.
4. Alcohol. It speeds onset but suppresses REM in the second half of the night, fragmenting sleep. Warn in advance that stopping often brings 2-3 worse nights before improvement.
5. Wind-down ritual starting ~35 minutes before bed: pick 2-3 items from the tiers in `references/sleep-protocol.md`.
6. Environment: dark (blackout, cover LEDs), cool (about 18-20 C / 65-68 F), steady low noise (fan or white noise; earplugs if a partner snores). Phone charges outside the bedroom.
7. Naps: only if night sleep is fine. Max 20 minutes, with an alarm, before ~3pm, in a chair rather than in bed. Skip naps entirely if there are sleep-onset problems.
8. Light: bright outdoor light soon after waking and dim light in the evening support the body clock (supported by circadian science; not in every source, but standard practice).

Do not add sleep restriction (limiting time in bed) as a self-directed step. It is a real CBT-I component but can cause daytime impairment; it belongs with a trained provider.

## Step 3: Burnout recovery sequence

Stabilize before restructuring. Reassess at week 6 against baseline.

| When | Focus | Core actions |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Stabilize | Screens off at a fixed time. No work email after a set evening hour. Brain dump for 10 min if awake, then close the notebook. "Absolute minimum": 3 work items that must happen this week, push/delegate/drop the rest. One recovery activity daily (slow outdoor walk, call someone you like, one non-productive activity). |
| Weeks 2-3 | Boundaries | Adapt the scripts in `references/boundary-scripts.md`. Track boundaries set vs. enforced. Keep a dated record of requests and responses (supports an HR escalation or leave case). |
| Weeks 3-4 | Energy audit | One full work week: tag each activity E / N / D (energizing, neutral, draining) by how it feels afterward. Find top 3 drains and top 3 energizers. Delegate or reduce drains, protect best-energy hours, restore dropped energizers. |
| Month 2+ | Meaning | Reflection questions over several sessions. Aim: enough meaning to offset the hard parts, not enjoyment of every minute. |
| Weeks 4-6 | Exit decision | Three questions: are the causes fixable, is the organization willing to change, have boundaries been respected? "No" to 2 or more means an environment problem: plan an exit rather than more individual recovery. |

Rules inside the sequence:
- Exercise and time outdoors are useful low-cost recovery activities; evidence for exercise on burnout is modest, so do not oversell it.
- Burnout from conditions an individual cannot change is not fixed by self-care. Be willing to say that.
- Before quitting without a plan: build 3+ months of financial runway where possible.
- Stress load framing: recovery requires reducing demands or raising resources, not only adding coping tactics. Check workload, control, reward, fairness and values fit when diagnosing causes (`references/burnout-screen-and-drivers.md`, `references/stress-load-management.md`).

## Step 4: Progress check

- Day 14 (sleep): repeat the audit; compare time to fall asleep, night wakings, morning feel, anchor adherence (days per week out of 7). No improvement means the escalation row above.
- Week 6 (burnout): repeat the three subscores; compare to baseline; run the exit decision.
- Separate timescales: sleep 7-14 days, active burnout recovery 4-6 weeks, full recovery often longer. Do not promise "recovered in 6 weeks".

## Evidence honesty (what not to claim)

- "Consistent wake time is the single strongest lever" is standard CBT-I practice, not a ranked finding from one study. State it as practice.
- Do not quote a fixed "blue light suppresses melatonin for 1-3 hours" number. Say: light-emitting screens used late can delay melatonin onset; dim, warm light and less screen use help.
- 4-7-8 breathing is an optional relaxation exercise, not an evidence-backed treatment.
- CBT-I has few side effects; do not say none (early sleep restriction can cause sleepiness).
- Do not state exercise is "the most effective" burnout intervention.
