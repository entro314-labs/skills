# Stress Load Management

Model: stress is load against recovery capacity. Burnout follows load that exceeds capacity for long periods without adequate recovery. Order of intervention: reduce load, increase recovery, then change appraisal.

## 1. Load inventory (one session)

1. List all current demands: work projects, meetings, caregiving, admin, social obligations.
2. Tag each:
   - Priority: must / should / optional.
   - Control: can change / can influence / cannot change.
3. Actions:
   - Optional + any control: drop or defer.
   - Should + can change: delegate, simplify, or renegotiate deadline.
   - Must + cannot change: plan recovery around it; accept it as a fixed cost.
4. Limit active must-do items per week (3 in the stabilization phase).

## 2. Recovery budget

| Level | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Daily | One recovery activity; a fixed shutdown time; some movement (about 15-20 min walk counts); the sleep window protected |
| Weekly | At least one half-day with no work thoughts or messages; one social contact that is not about work; one activity done for its own sake |
| After a peak | A lighter day or two deliberately scheduled; do not follow a crunch with another crunch |

## 3. Daily tracking (30 seconds)

- Morning feel 1-5.
- Stress/energy 1-5 in the evening.
- Review 7-day trends. A single bad day is noise; a downward trend over a week is a signal to cut load.

## 4. Acute regulation tools (support, not substitute)

- Slow breathing: for example 4-7-8 breathing (inhale 4 s, hold 7 s, exhale 8 s, 3 cycles) or a longer exhale than inhale.
- 10-minute written brain dump, then stop.
- Short outdoor walk; movement lowers acute arousal.
- Use these to get through a moment; if they are the only intervention while load stays the same, expect relapse.

## 5. Appraisal

- Separate "cannot control" from "can influence" in writing.
- Reframe inefficacy thoughts against load: "I'm doing a 60-hour job in 40 hours" is a capacity fact, not a competence failure.
- Normalize: burnout is a response to sustained conditions, not a personal failure.

## 6. Connections between sleep and stress

- Poor sleep raises reactivity and lowers coping; protect the wake anchor even in busy weeks.
- Late-evening work and email extend arousal into the night; enforce the work cutoff (about 19:00 email, about 22:00 screens in the stabilization phase).
- If stress persists with good sleep and reduced load after several weeks, escalate to a clinician.
