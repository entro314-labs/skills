# Evidence Notes

How firmly to state each claim when coaching.

| Claim | Strength | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| CBT-I is first-line for chronic insomnia over medication | Strong (guideline-level, from internal-medicine and sleep-medicine bodies) | "Behavioural therapy for insomnia is the recommended first treatment." |
| Stimulus control (leave bed after about 20 min awake) | Strong (core CBT-I component) | State directly |
| Caffeine 6 h before bed reduces sleep | Moderate (single controlled study; the "about an hour" figure is unverified) | "Caffeine even 6 hours before bed can cost sleep." Do not quote a number. |
| Caffeine half-life 5-7 h | Good | Use 6 h; longer for over 50, some medications, slow metabolizers |
| Bedtime minus 12 h cutoff | Practical rule derived from two half-lives | Present as a rule of thumb, not a clinical threshold; 6 h is a central half-life estimate |
| Alcohol fragments sleep, suppresses REM later in the night | Good | State directly |
| Blue-wavelength light suppresses melatonin; wavelength matters more than brightness | Good | Avoid quoting a fixed "1-3 hour" window |
| Fixed wake time stabilises sleep drive and circadian timing | Good mechanism; "strongest single lever" is not supported | State the practice and mechanism, not a ranking |
| 30-minute weekend variation threshold, 90-minute lie-in as jet lag, bedtime self-corrects in 1-2 weeks | Heuristic, not trial results | "A useful rule of thumb" |
| Warm shower or bath helps onset via later body-temperature drop | Moderate (body-heating studies; benefit seen about 1-2 h before bed) | Schedule 1-2 h before bed, not in the last 20 min; mechanism, not guarantee |
| 4-7-8 breathing for sleep | Weak | Optional relaxation technique |
| Coffee-nap | Modest | "Some people find it helps"; do not cite sleep-inertia work as its source |
| Low control is a major strain factor (job demand-control model: high demands plus low control) | Good for strain; not a burnout ranking | "Low control over your work adds to strain." Do not claim it beats workload. |
| Exercise helps burnout | Moderate-good | "One of the better-supported interventions", not "the single most effective" |
| Burnout and depression co-occur often | Good | Recommend clinical assessment when low mood persists |
| Three-dimension model: exhaustion, cynicism, inefficacy | Established framework | State directly |
| 12-24 / 25-40 / 41-60 total bands | Coaching heuristic only | Never call them inventory cut-offs; the licensed inventory scores subscales separately |
| Recovery takes "about 4-6 weeks" | Heuristic | Say "often several weeks; varies widely, and longer if the cause remains" |

Reference-quality cautions:
- Do not fabricate or cite DOIs and journal details from memory in client-facing text; describe the finding instead.
- Referral resources are region-specific (crisis lines, insurance coverage of home sleep tests, EAP availability). Ask the user's region and localise.
