# Burnout Recovery Plan

Recovery is non-linear and takes several weeks at minimum. Bad days are expected. Assess severity first (see screen reference) and run one phase at a time. Do not promise a fixed timeline.

## Week 1: stabilize

- Reduce to 3 must-do items for the week. Delegate, defer, or drop everything else.
- Screens off at 22:00. No work email after 19:00.
- If unable to sleep: write thoughts for 10 min, then stop. Start the sleep anchor (see sleep protocol).
- One recovery activity per day: a slow 20 min walk, a call with someone you like, or one non-productive activity.
- Physical activity, including short walks, is linked to lower burnout in small studies. Present it as supportive, not as the single most effective fix.

Checkpoint: Did the three-item list survive the week? Did sleep timing hold?

## Weeks 2-3: boundaries

Use the scripts and trade-off method in boundary-scripts.md (extra work, off-hours, meetings, scope, deadlines, manager escalation). One boundary per week, lowest stakes first; expect pushback and prepare a follow-up line.

- Practice with the user in the session; rehearse the first 2 sentences and the follow-up to pushback.
- Record boundary violations: date, request, response. This supports HR escalation or a medical-leave case.

## Weeks 3-4: energy audit

1. For one work week, log each activity and tag it E (energizing), N (neutral), D (draining).
2. Pick the top 3 drains and top 3 energizers.
3. For each drain: delegate, batch, shorten, or reduce contact.
4. Protect peak-energy hours for the hardest or most meaningful work.
5. Restore energizers that were dropped (hobby, mentoring, exercise, specific colleagues).

## Month 2 and beyond: meaning

Over several sessions, not one. Questions:
- What would you still do if money were not an issue?
- When were you last proud of your work?
- Which part of your work helps someone?

Goal: enough meaning to offset the hard parts, not enjoying every minute. Do not run this phase while the person is still exhausted; meaning work on an empty tank reads as pressure.

## Weeks 4-6: decision point

Check these, honestly:
- Are the causes fixable (workload, control, reward, community, fairness, values)?
- Is the organization willing to change?
- Have the boundaries held?

If 2 or more are "no", treat it as an environment problem and plan an exit:
- Build 3+ months of runway before leaving.
- Use the boundary violation log for HR escalation or a medical-leave discussion if relevant.
- Do not make a resignation decision from an exhausted state if it can wait for stabilization.

## Escalation

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Symptoms worse after 4-6 weeks | See a primary care doctor; burnout can coexist with clinical depression; ask HR about employer-funded counseling |
| Suicidal thoughts | Medical situation. US: call or text 988. Elsewhere: local crisis line or emergency number |
| Cost is a barrier | Suggest sliding-scale therapy, community clinics, peer support; check local options |
| Severe screen result | Therapy, medical leave discussion, or major change; not DIY coaching alone |

## Coaching rules

- Never say "push through".
- Do not make recovery another performance task; keep it small and daily.
- Acknowledge that systemic problems (overload, unfairness) are not fixed by the individual alone.
- Check in weekly during the first month; revisit sleep adherence at each check-in because it is the fastest leading indicator.
