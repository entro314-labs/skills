# Sleep Audit, Red Flags, Escalation, Tracking

## Audit (ask one question at a time)

1. Usual bedtime
2. Weekday wake time (and weekend wake time)
3. Time to fall asleep
4. Night wakings (count, duration)
5. Morning feel, 1-5
6. Alcohol to fall asleep
7. Screens in bed
8. Last caffeine of the day
9. Naps
10. Bedroom dark, cool, quiet

Also ask about snoring or gasping (partner-reported), shift work or overnight caregiving, and daytime sleepiness.

## Red flags and what each means

| Finding | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep onset under 5 min | Possible chronic sleep deprivation | Extend sleep opportunity via the anchor and earlier wind-down; check total hours; if morning feel is also 2 or lower, treat as a sleep-deprivation flag |
| Waking unrefreshed regardless of duration | Possible sleep apnea | Refer to a doctor; a home sleep study is often an option and may be covered by insurance |
| Snoring or gasping | Possible sleep apnea | Same; always flag snoring plus unrefreshed waking as a medical issue |
| Alcohol as sleep aid | Sleep fragmentation, REM suppression | Address first; warn about 2-3 worse nights after stopping |
| Racing thoughts at bedtime | Anxiety-driven | Brain dump earlier in evening; CBT-I or therapy if persistent |
| Shift work, overnight caregiving, regular cross-timezone travel | Circadian disruption | Outside standard hygiene; needs circadian management |
| Caffeine after 14:00 | Likely contributor | Move the cutoff per the 12 h rule |
| Weekend wake time more than about 90 min later | Social jet lag | Re-anchor |

Naming note: keep the sleep-deprivation flag (onset under 5 min plus morning feel 2 or lower) separate from the sleep-apnea flag (snoring, gasping, unrefreshed waking). Do not label the first as apnea.

## Plan building

1. Set the wake anchor (week 1 priority).
2. Add the environment fix (darkness, temperature).
3. Add 2-3 wind-down items.
4. Set the caffeine cutoff.
5. Address alcohol and naps if relevant.

Introduce over 1-2 weeks; do not stack everything at once for a struggling person.

## Escalation ladder

1. Hygiene and anchor for 14 days.
2. Day-14 re-audit. 2+ of onset, wakings, morning feel, anchor adherence not improved: refer for sleep apnea screening or CBT-I.
3. CBT-I is first-line over sleep medication for chronic insomnia. Point to a provider directory run by a behavioral sleep medicine professional society.
4. Never recommend prescription sleep medication within this protocol.
5. If the person improved, keep the anchor and relax lower-value items one at a time.

## Tracking state (if the system persists data)

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Audit answers | The 10 audit fields |
| sleep_anchor_time | Fixed wake time |
| anchor_adherence_days | Count of days within tolerance |
| wind_down_ritual | The chosen 2-3 steps per tier |
| caffeine_cutoff_time | Computed from bedtime minus 12 h |
| protocol_start_date | Day 0 for the day-14 check |
| checkin_scores | Morning feel 1-5 over time |
| flags | possible_sleep_apnea, alcohol_use, anxiety_driven, sleep_deprivation |

## Reminder set

| When | Content |
|---|---|
| Morning, at anchor | Get up, light exposure |
| Bedtime minus 35 min | Start wind-down |
| Bedtime minus 10 min | Screens off, phone out |
| Day 14 | Re-audit and compare |

## Adherence tips

- Tell the user in advance that the first week may feel no better, and that alcohol removal may temporarily worsen sleep.
- If adherence falls, reduce the plan to the anchor alone before adding anything.
- Do not interpret a single bad night as protocol failure; keep the anchor.
