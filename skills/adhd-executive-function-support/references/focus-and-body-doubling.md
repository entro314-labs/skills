# Focus Sessions and Body Doubling

Body doubling is working alongside another person (in the room or on a shared call) whose passive presence provides external accountability. For an AI copilot it means a structured session: commit to a micro-step, work, report back.

## Ownership of time (read first)

An AI cannot keep time between turns. Fixed check-in marks are the user's job:
- The user sets the timer(s) (phone, browser, visual countdown). Never promise to ping at minute 15.
- When the user returns (or at the end), ask the check-in question.
- Never run timers, shell commands or system notifications on the user's behalf.
- Suggest a human body double (peer, shared co-working call, library) when presence matters; the goal is passive presence, not conversation.

## Session start protocol

Combine into one or two messages; do not interrogate (focus mode allows at most two exchanges before the timer starts).
1. What task?
2. What is the first step? It must take under 2 minutes.
3. Shrink it: "What is the smallest possible version of that?"
4. Commit: "Do that now, report back when done."
5. Validate; ask for the next micro-step.

Propose the first step yourself if they cannot name it; stage 2-3 setup actions (water, close tabs, headphones).

## Session types

| Type | Length | Check-ins (user timer) | End |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick fire | 25 min | 1 at 15 min | Hard stop at 25 |
| Deep dive | 50 min | At 15 and 35 min | 10 min break |
| Sprint | 90 min as 3 x 25 min | Every 20-25 min | Break between blocks |
| Extended | 2+ hours | Every 20-25 min | Mandatory breaks between blocks |

Guidelines: never go more than about 25 minutes without contact in an accountability session (practical heuristic). A 15-minute session checks at 10 minutes.

## Check-in questions
- What micro-step did you just complete?
- Where are you right now, specifically?
- What is the next micro-step?
- Did that move the needle, or just feel productive?

## Push-back on vague answers (accountability mode only)
Use only when the person has asked for accountability; keep the tone matter-of-fact, never contemptuous.
- "What SPECIFICALLY did you do?"
- "Name the exact blocker."
- "Is that a reason or an excuse?" / "Are you avoiding this, or is it a real blocker?"
- "Tell me the EXACT next action, not the general area."
Follow-ups for "stuck": "What PART is hard?", "Which distractions?", "What have you actually tried?", "What would 'working' look like?"

## Reality check when there is no progress
- "What do you actually want to do right now?"
- "Is this task real, or are you punishing yourself?"
- "Is this resistance, or do you need rest?"
- "What's the worst that happens if you stop?"
If rest is the answer, end the session with credit for showing up.

## Resets (2-5 min): pick ONE, do it, return

| Reset | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical | Jumping jacks, stretch, short walk |
| Sensory swap | Change environment or music |
| Micro-win | One tiny task |
| External input | 1 minute of motivating content (timed) |
| Brain dump | 2 minutes writing everything out |
| Hydrate | Water |
| Permission slip | "5 minutes of nothing, then back" |

Emergency reset when fully blocked: stop 30 seconds (hands off keyboard); 3 breaths; "what ONE thing am I avoiding?"; shrink the task 10x; promise 5 minutes; go. If still blocked after 5 minutes, end the session without judgment.

## Reward menu (choose BEFORE starting)

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Movement | Walk, stretch, one song |
| Sensory | Coffee, snack, blanket |
| Social | Text a friend, time-boxed social check |
| Creation | Doodle, music, tidy |
| Nature | Step outside |
| Novelty | Short read or video |
| Completion | Check off the task (sometimes the reward by itself) |

Temptation bundling (pairing a task with something enjoyable, such as music or a favorite drink) lowers the cost of starting. Keep rewards time-boxed so they do not become the next derailment.

## Session closure
Acknowledge what moved, however small. Ask: keep going, switch, or done? Record the next micro-step for next time so the next start is already shrunk. Offer a shutdown ritual at end of day.

## Session autopsy (process, not performance)
1. What worked? 2. What did not? 3. One thing for next time. 4. What was actually accomplished (small wins included).
Optionally note energy at start and end (e.g. 7/10 to 5/10). If sessions are logged (planned vs actual duration, task type, time of day, first micro-step, resets used), review monthly to find the best session length and time of day.
