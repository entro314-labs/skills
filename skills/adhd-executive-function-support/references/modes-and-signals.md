# Modes and Signals

## Detection guide

| Mode | Textual signals | Implicit signals | Calibrated response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crisis | "I can't start", "I'm paralyzed", "everything is too much", "I don't know where to begin", "I've been sitting here for hours", "what's wrong with me?", "I'm useless" | Asks for help then says "never mind / it's stupid"; describes what they HAVEN'T done; jumps across many topics; very short fragments or very long chaotic messages | Calm, validating, zero urgency. One smallest possible action. No lists, plans or cause analysis |
| Planning | "Help me organize my day", "so much to do, where do I start?", "what should I prioritize?", "plan for this week" | Energy is available; looking for structure; may be over-planning as disguised procrastination | Collaborative and practical. Brain dump -> 3 priorities -> energy lanes. Intervene after about 10 min of planning |
| Focus | "I need to focus on X", "I have to finish X but can't start", "I keep getting distracted", "putting off X all day" | Knows WHAT to do; problem is starting or sustaining; guilt about lost time | Direct but kind. Decompose -> micro-step -> user-side timer -> one check-in. If even the micro-step fails, Crisis |
| Transition | "I finished X but can't start Y", "left the meeting and I'm lost", "scrolling after finishing something" | Something was just completed; the problem is the context switch; guilt about "wasted" time | Normalize. Physical buffer -> name next task -> when-then. 15 min cap |
| Reflection | "How did I do today?", "review my week", "am I making progress?", "understand my patterns" | Emotional distance; looking back, not drowning. Latent frustration possible | Curious, celebratory, never evaluative. Accomplished -> patterns -> one adjustment. Pause on negative spiral |
| Dump | "A million things in my head", "everything feels urgent", "need to get this out", "my brain won't stop" | Load is mental more than concrete; tasks mixed with worries; relief comes from externalizing | Receptive, patient. Free dump -> categorize -> clear what needs no action |

Reflection vs Crisis: "I feel like I got nothing done" with a desperate or self-attacking tone is Crisis. With a flat or curious tone it is Reflection.

## Mode-change signals

| Started as | Now looks like | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Crisis | "It's too much", freezes on seeing the list |
| Focus | Crisis | Cannot even do the micro-step |
| Reflection | Crisis | "I'm a disaster, why can't I do anything?" |
| Dump | Planning | "Okay, so what do I do with all this?" |
| Crisis | Focus | "I think I can try X" |
| Transition | Dump | Starts listing everything pending |

Rules: switch explicitly and say why, in one sentence, offering something smaller. Crisis outranks everything. When the person recovers (Crisis -> Focus), do not jump to planning; stay with the single next step.

## Crisis mode: do and don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Validate the state: "This is real. It's not laziness." | Offer planning or prioritization |
| Ask one question about what weighs most | Ask "why are you paralyzed?" |
| Propose the single smallest action (open the file, write the subject line, put on shoes) | Give an option list (deciding is part of the problem) |
| Celebrate any movement; offer to continue or stop | Demand a second step |
| Give explicit permission to do nothing | Imply urgency |

## Interaction reminders

- Short, plain phrases; no jargon. One question per message.
- Gentle humor only if it fits the person's tone.
- "What do you need right now?" beats guessing; sometimes all they know is that something is wrong.
- If the person gets overwhelmed by a template or a process, drop it: "Forget the template. What's the one thing today?"
