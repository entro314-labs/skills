# Plain-English substitutions and cuts

These pairs are stable, but a plain-English table is a style choice, not a rule: keep a term when the audience expects it (legal, regulated or technical text).

## Delete outright
"at the end of the day", "as a matter of fact", "it should be understood", "to all intents and purposes", "last but not least", "the fact of the matter is", "in due course" (say when), "I am of the opinion that" (use "I think").

Filler adverbs and intensifiers: absolutely, actually, basically, obviously, of course, quite, really, very, extremely, incredibly, just, literally, honestly, totally, definitely, certainly, clearly, rather, somewhat, fairly, pretty, simply, truly, genuinely, essentially.

## Structural cuts
- Optional "that"; "things" and "stuff" (name the thing).
- Nominalizations: "make a decision" -> "decide"; "provide assistance" -> "help".
- Passive -> active when the actor matters: "Reports are generated by the tool" -> "The tool generates reports".
