# Copy Editing and Review

Edit, do not rewrite. The job is to make existing copy clearer, more specific, more credible and lower-friction while keeping the author's message and voice.

## Core rules (in order of importance)

1. **Know goal, audience and desired action before editing.** If missing, ask: goal (awareness, conversion, retention), target action, audience, brand voice, known concerns, available proof. Read any existing product/brand context file in the project first; a brand voice guide, if present, overrides your taste on voice, tone and style. Project style rules beat defaults.
2. **Read once without editing.** Mark problems, do not fix on the first read.
3. **One dimension per pass.** Multi-concern edits hide problems. Use the seven sweeps below.
4. **Never change the core message.** Never alter quotes or testimonials. Every edit states its reason (a principle, not a preference).
5. **Never invent proof.** Sample numbers in examples are illustrative. If no real number, fact or testimonial exists, soften the claim or ask the author. Why: fabricated stats are the fastest way to destroy trust and create legal exposure.
6. **Prioritize by conversion impact over writing taste.** Order: value proposition and headline, CTA clarity, missing proof, specificity, then grammar and polish.
7. **Deliver collaboratively.** Present findings sweep by sweep (not one dump), propose concrete edits, let the author decide, then re-check earlier sweeps. Inline rewrites of the author's file only when permitted.

## The Seven Sweeps

Run in this order. Rationale: structural problems (clarity) must be fixed before tone, and persuasion layers (proof, emotion, risk) only work on clear, specific copy.

| # | Sweep | Question | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clarity | Understood on first read? | Split sentences, name the pronoun's referent, explain or cut jargon, supply missing context. One main idea per section ("Rule of One"); copy addresses the reader ("you", not "we/the company"). |
| 2 | Voice and tone | Same personality throughout? | Unify formality, "we" vs "the company", mood shifts. Read aloud. |
| 3 | So what | Why should the reader care? | After each claim ask "so what?"; add a "which means..." bridge from feature to outcome. |
| 4 | Prove it | Is each claim substantiated? | Add named testimonial, number with source, case study, third-party validation, guarantee, logos, review score; otherwise soften. |
| 5 | Specificity | Concrete or vague? | Replace "improve/enhance/optimize" with numbers, timeframes, examples; delete what cannot be made specific. |
| 6 | Heightened emotion | Does it make the reader feel anything? | Name the pain of the current state, use sensory language, micro-stories, reflective questions. Emotion serves the message; no manipulation. |
| 7 | Zero risk | What would make the reader hesitate near the CTA? | List every hesitation; address each: guarantee, free trial, no card required, cancel anytime, proof next to CTA, stated next step, privacy assurance, no hidden costs. |

Return path after each sweep (re-check earlier dimensions, because later edits break earlier fixes): after 2 -> 1; after 3 -> 2, 1; after 4 -> 3, 2, 1; after 5 -> 4, 3, 2, 1; after 6 -> 5 ... 1; after 7 -> 6 ... 1 (full reverse). Stop when a full pass finds nothing new. Details and examples: `references/seven-sweeps.md`.

## Quick pass (when a full review is not warranted)

- Cut: very, really, extremely, incredibly, just, actually, basically, "in order to" (-> "to"), optional "that", "things", "stuff".
- Replace: utilize -> use; implement -> set up; leverage -> use; facilitate -> help; innovative -> new; robust -> strong; seamless -> smooth; cutting-edge -> new/modern.
- Convert passive to active; convert nominalizations ("make a decision" -> "decide").
- Sentences: one idea each, front-load the point, vary length, about 25 words is a ceiling (aim under 20 for web), at most about 3 conjunctions.
- Paragraphs: one topic, 2-4 sentences on web, strong first sentence.
- Longer plain-English substitution list: `references/plain-english-and-cuts.md`.

## Common copy problems

| Problem | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wall of features | Says what it does, not why it matters | "which means..." after each feature |
| Corporate speak | "Leverage synergies to optimize outcomes" | Ask how a person would say it |
| Weak opening | Company history, vague claim | Lead with reader's problem or outcome |
| Buried or vague CTA | Ask is late or unclear | Obvious, early, repeated; verb plus benefit |
| No proof | "Customers love us" | Named testimonials, numbers, case references |
| Generic claim | "We help businesses grow" | Who, how, by how much |
| Mixed audience | Speaks to everyone | Pick one audience |
| Feature overload | Every capability listed | Keep 3-5 benefits that matter to that audience |

Flag proactively, without being asked:
- No stated goal or audience: ask before starting.
- Multiple tone shifts: quote the exact lines under Voice.
- Features outnumber benefits 2:1 or more: raise So What first.
- Superlatives without proof ("best", "leading", "most trusted"): flag each under Prove It.
- Vague or buried CTA: call out first, under Zero Risk.

## Measuring: readability and scoring

- Readability targets (heuristics, not standards): Flesch Reading Ease 60-80 for most marketing copy; Flesch-Kincaid grade about 6-8 for general audiences; passive voice under 10% of sentences; adverbs under 5% of words; 0-3 filler words per 100 words; sentences under ~20 words. Automated counts are approximate (syllable heuristics, passive-voice false positives, sentence-splitting errors): use them for trends and before/after comparison, never as pass/fail. Formulas and pitfalls: `references/readability-metrics.md`.
- Weighted scoring of a piece (hook 25, emotional resonance 25, technique 20, specificity 15, CTA 10, compliance 5) is a design choice, not validated evidence. Use it to compare variants relatively. Spec, compliance-check fix and output contract: `references/scoring-and-page-rubric.md`.
- Page-level audit: seven categories (value proposition, clarity, persuasion, CTAs, microcopy, tone, grammar), each graded A-D, strict standard. Rubric and audit workflow: `references/scoring-and-page-rubric.md`.

## Review modes and reporting

Choose the output that matches the ask:

| Request | Output |
|---|---|
| Full review | Seven-sweep report with proposed edits |
| Quick pass | Word- and sentence-level edits |
| Checklist run | Pass/fail per section plus priority fixes |
| Single sweep | Focused before/after examples |
| Final polish | Clean copy plus list of changes made |
| Line-by-line audit | Structured table (Location, Original, Issue/rule, Suggested fix) |
| Quick scan | Top 5 issues only |

Reporting conventions: bottom line first (overall copy health), then each issue as what is wrong, why it hurts conversion, how to fix it. Tag each finding: clear improvement, judgment call, or needs author input. Quote original text verbatim. For subjective tone choices offer two options instead of picking silently. In analysis-only tasks stay read-only; apply fixes only when asked. Four-phase review method, report template and AI-writing tells: `references/review-report.md`.

## Final checklist

- Goal, audience and desired action known; read once unedited.
- Clarity: first-read comprehension, jargon explained, pronouns clear.
- Voice: formality consistent, no mood jumps, reads well aloud.
- So what: every feature tied to a benefit.
- Prove it: claims substantiated, proof specific and attributed, no unearned superlatives.
- Specificity: vague words replaced, numbers and timeframes present, filler gone.
- Emotion: pain feels real, aspiration feels reachable.
- Zero risk: objections answered near CTA, next step explicit, risk reversal stated.
- No typos, consistent formatting and terminology, no placeholder or broken interpolation (`{name}`, lorem ipsum), links work, core message unchanged.
