# Brand Voice

Voice is identity made audible — the blend of experience, conviction, and rhythm that tells readers who's speaking and why it matters.

**Voice vs. tone:** you have the same voice with your boss and your friends, but your tone changes. Voice = consistent personality; tone = how it adapts to context.

**Voice vs. messaging vs. positioning:** positioning answers "why choose us?" (strategy), messaging answers "what do we say?" (content), voice answers "how do we say it?" (applied everywhere).

This skill has two modes: **developing** a voice (frameworks + documentation) and **preserving** a voice (keeping AI-assisted content authentic).

---

## Mode 1: Developing a Brand Voice

### Discovery frameworks (pick 1–2, don't stack all)

**BrandSort (Margot Bloomstein):** sort attribute cards into "who you are" / "who you'd like to be" / "who you're NOT". Prioritize 8–12 aspirational attributes, then unpack abstract terms ("innovative" → concrete behaviors). The NOT pile is as valuable as the others. Balance user needs with brand identity — purely user-focused voices converge on bland sameness.

**NN/g Four Dimensions of Tone:** plot the brand on Formality (formal↔casual), Humor (serious↔funny), Reverence (respectful↔irreverent), Energy (matter-of-fact↔enthusiastic). A brand can be serious AND casual. Avoid landing in the middle of every dimension — that's generic. (Research note: casual, conversational tone measurably increases trustworthiness.)

**Aaker Brand Personality:** identify 1–2 primary dimensions as a starting point — Sincerity, Excitement, Competence, Sophistication, Ruggedness.

**Brand-as-person exercise:** What would they do for work? Wear? Never say? Who would they befriend? What annoys them?

### Refinement: "This but not that" (Slack's technique)

The "not that" constraint prevents misinterpretation:

| Attribute | Constraint |
|-----------|------------|
| Confident | but not cocky |
| Witty | but not silly |
| Friendly | but not ingratiating |
| Conversational | but always appropriate |

Define every voice attribute this way, with a do/don't example pair each.

### Tone adaptation matrices

**By channel:** social (more casual, playful) · marketing (confident, benefit-focused) · support (empathetic, clear) · legal (formal, precise) · product UI (concise, action-oriented).

**By reader's emotional state (Mailchimp's approach):** happy → match energy, celebrate · confused → patient, clear · frustrated → empathy first, then help · anxious → reassuring, concrete.

### Documentation: the 8-component verbal identity

1. **Voice attributes** — 3–5 traits with definitions + "this but not that"
2. **Tone guidelines** — adaptation across contexts, channels, emotional states
3. **Messaging architecture** — link to positioning-messaging output
4. **Naming conventions** — product/feature naming system
5. **Verbal distinctive assets** — ownable phrases, signature words
6. **Vocabulary guidelines** — words to use/avoid, jargon policy, inclusive language
7. **Grammar & mechanics** — contractions, punctuation, emoji policy
8. **Examples library** — before/after rewrites, good/bad pairs (the most-used part; invest here)

Include an **AI-usage section**: the 3–5 attributes distilled with explanations, extensive examples, and ready-to-use prompts — voice guidelines that don't work as AI instructions go stale immediately.

### Validation tests

| Test | Pass criteria |
|------|---------------|
| **Airport test** | Recognizable without the logo |
| **Consistency test** | Translates across every touchpoint |
| **Audience test** | Resonates with actual customers |
| **Authenticity test** | Not forced or performative |
| **Differentiation test** | Distinct from competitors (don't copy Oatly) |

### Anti-patterns

Generic attribute lists ("innovative, customer-focused, passionate") · vague guidelines ("be friendly") without dos/don'ts · voice reflecting the company instead of researched audience preferences · create-then-forget with no audits or examples upkeep.

## Mode 2: Preserving Voice in AI-Assisted Writing

For mission-critical content, use AI as collaborator, not ghostwriter: the human's messy draft contains the voice; AI polish can preserve it, AI generation replaces it.

### The five-step preservation workflow

1. **Free-write intent** (human) — say it messily, no editing
2. **Clarify the reader's question** — what are they worried about, hoping for, skeptical of?
3. **AI organizes, doesn't invent** — "Restructure this draft for clarity and flow, but keep my tone and phrasing wherever possible. Highlight jargon or clichés that weaken authenticity."
4. **Reclaim emotional beats** — re-insert the author's idioms, stories, specifics
5. **Read-aloud test** — if you couldn't say it in a meeting, rewrite until you could

### Voice audit: generic tells vs. voice signals

**Flag for deletion** (could appear on any competitor's site unchanged):
- Vague value words: innovative, cutting-edge, holistic, robust, passionate (without evidence)
- Corporate filler: leverage, synergize, thought leadership, moving forward
- Empty intensifiers: very, truly, unprecedented, groundbreaking
- Generic openers: "We believe in…", "We are committed to…", "Our mission is to…"

**Preserve** (genuine identity markers):
- Specific stories: named people, dates, concrete outcomes with numbers, real quotes
- Conviction signals: positions someone could disagree with, admissions of limitation, contrarian takes
- Natural speech: varied sentence lengths, contractions, fragments for emphasis, real questions

### Quality tests

- **Substitution test**: swap in a competitor's name — does the sentence still work? If yes, it lacks voice.
- **Meeting test**: could you say this to your team without feeling scripted?
- **Bar test**: could you explain it to a smart friend at a bar?
- **Memory test**: if the reader keeps one sentence, is it the one you want — and is it prominent?

### Voice recovery techniques (when a draft has no voice)

- **Interview method**: answer the document's question out loud, transcribe, use as draft
- **Letter method**: rewrite as a personal letter to one specific person, then generalize minimally
- **Story anchor**: start from a specific moment that happened; build outward
- **Negative space**: list what you're NOT saying and positions you reject; sharpen against them

### Division of labor

| AI handles | Human provides |
|-----------|----------------|
| Restructuring for clarity | The messy first draft with authentic voice |
| Flagging jargon and clichés | Judgment on which phrases ARE the voice |
| Checking logical flow | Emotional beats and personal stories |
| Generating alternatives to consider | Final selection; the read-aloud verdict |
| Cutting 15–25% for brevity | Positions requiring conviction |
