# Press, Launch and Specialty Copy

Shared principle: this copy is read by a gatekeeper (editor, store reviewer, browsing reader) who decides in seconds. Lead with the news or the promise, back every claim with a fact, cut anything that does not earn its place.

## Press release workflow

1. **Intake (ask only for gaps).** The news; release type; audience (trade or general); region; media format; company facts (name, what it does, HQ, key figures); spokesperson and quote message; supporting data; embargo (date, time, timezone); language. Nice to have: boilerplate, contact, multimedia, distribution plan.
2. **Angle.** State the news in one sentence. Test it against news values: impact, timeliness, prominence, novelty, proximity. If weak, say so and propose a stronger angle.
3. **Headlines.** Offer 5-10, varying the hook type (data, question, bold claim, contrast, human interest, urgency, counterintuitive) and label each. Get a choice before drafting.
4. **Draft** to the structure below. Count the lead words.
5. **Deliver** the release plus a short note on the chosen angle. Offer next steps: send window, pitch email with subject-line variants, social teaser, journalist shortlist criteria.
6. Optional de-AI pass (remove inflated language, rhythmic sameness, hollow transitions) but freeze headline and lead; loosening them breaks the word count and the pyramid.

## Structure (wire / AP-style release)

Order: release designation (`FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE` or `EMBARGO <date, time, timezone>`), headline, optional subhead, dateline + lead, body 1 (primary data), quote 1 (senior exec), body 2 (context, market data), quote 2 (optional, third party), body 3 (availability, CTA), boilerplate, media contact, end mark `###` (or `-30-`).

- **Inverted pyramid.** Every paragraph must be removable from the bottom without losing the core message; editors cut from the bottom.
- **Lead.** Answers who/what/when/where/why/how in 25-35 words. Count them. Under 25 is thin, over 35 buries the news.
- **News test.** If sentence one contains no news, rewrite. "Company X, a leader in..." is not news; "Company X today launched..." is.
- **Headline.** 10-15 words, present tense, active voice, no period, no exclamation point, no superlatives, key news present. Case: follow the target outlet's house style and say which you used; default to sentence case for AP-style US/Canada releases (title case only where a wire house style requires it).
- **Subhead.** About 15-25 words: secondary angle, data point or differentiator.
- **Length.** Default 300-500 words total; 400-600 acceptable for Latin America; 500-700 for technical trade releases. Over 800 is skimmed or ignored.
- **One release, one story.** Two announcements means two releases.
- **Voice.** Third person throughout; no we/our/us/you outside direct quotes. Active voice. Write so a general-assignment reporter understands paragraph one; trade jargon only in trade releases, after the news is established.
- **Data beats adjectives.** "Revenue grew 47% year over year to EUR 12M", not "grew substantially". Every claim carries a number, a source or a quote. State the real price, not "competitive pricing".

## Quotes and attribution

- Use "said" only (not stated, commented, shared, expressed, noted). Full name and title on first reference, last name after. Present-tense "says" only for ongoing situations.
- Format: `"Quote," said Full Name, Title at Company.`
- One or two quotes (executive/founder plus partner or customer), 2-3 sentences each, never opening with "I".
- A quote must give what the reporter cannot write: opinion, vision, context. It must not restate the lead.
  - Bad: "We are excited to announce this partnership," said the CEO.
  - Good: "This changes how European retailers handle returns. Our pilot showed a 40% reduction in processing time," said [Name], CEO of [Company].

## Banned phrases

thrilled, excited to announce, proud to, innovative, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, synergy, leverage, disruptive, game-changing, revolutionary, groundbreaking, paradigm shift, next-generation, "leading" (unless backed by market-share data), "competitive pricing".

## AP quick rules

| Rule | Form |
|---|---|
| Numbers | one through nine spelled out; 10+ digits; always numerals for ages, percentages, money |
| Percent | "percent" in body text, not "%" |
| Titles | before name capitalised ("CEO Sarah Chen"); after name lowercase ("Sarah Chen, chief executive officer") |
| Company suffix | no Inc./Corp. in body ("Company X") |
| Serial comma | none in AP-style text |
| Times | "a.m." / "p.m." lowercase with periods |
| States | AP abbreviations (Calif., not CA); never abbreviated: Alaska, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Ohio, Texas, Utah |
| Dateline | `SAN FRANCISCO, Jan. 15, 2026 -` ; month abbreviations Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.; March, April, May, June, July spelled out |

Stand-alone US datelines (no state): Atlanta, Baltimore, Boston, Chicago, Cincinnati, Cleveland, Dallas, Denver, Detroit, Honolulu, Houston, Indianapolis, Las Vegas, Los Angeles, Miami, Milwaukee, Minneapolis, Nashville, New Orleans, New York, Oklahoma City, Philadelphia, Phoenix, Pittsburgh, St. Louis, Salt Lake City, San Antonio, San Diego, San Francisco, Seattle. Do not mix the AP dateline form with the "CITY, State (Month Day, Year) --" form in one document; follow the target market's table in references/regional-conventions.md.

## Boilerplate and contact

- Boilerplate: 3-4 factual sentences, no superlatives, identical across releases. "[Company] is a [category] company that [what] for [who]. Founded in [year] and headquartered in [city], it serves [number] customers across [markets]. For more information, visit [URL]."
- Media contact: name, title, email, phone; a dedicated person for crises.

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Headline: no period/superlatives/exclamation, key news present, case style stated
- [ ] Lead 5W1H in 25-35 words, counted
- [ ] Each paragraph cuttable from the bottom
- [ ] Third person, active voice, no banned phrases
- [ ] "said" only; quotes add insight, none starts with "I"
- [ ] At least one concrete number; every claim checked
- [ ] Dateline and style match the target region
- [ ] Boilerplate (3-4 sentences), media contact, end mark
- [ ] Embargo (if any) agreed with each recipient before sending

## Embargoes and timing

- An embargo is an agreement. Get each journalist's explicit consent before sending embargoed material; without consent there is no embargo. Remind 24 hours before it lifts; if it breaks, release to everyone immediately.
- Default send window: Tuesday-Thursday, mid-morning local time. Avoid Monday morning, Friday afternoon, lunch hours, national holidays. Regional exceptions in references/regional-conventions.md.
- Funding: early in the week. Product launch: embargoed briefings 2-3 weeks ahead, release on launch day. Earnings: outside trading hours.
- Crisis: holding statement immediately, never "no comment", never a Friday dump.

## Common mistakes

Superlatives (editors ignore them); exclamation points; burying the lead in paragraph three; over-length; jargon for a general audience; no fact-check; no media contact; an unknown-body "innovation award" as the news; vague plans ("expand internationally") instead of concrete ones ("open a Paris office in Q1 2026").

## Specialty copy: rules that carry across formats

- **Promise an experience, not a summary.** Books, events and listings sell the outcome and the stakes; plot, agenda or feature lists support it.
- **One core value proposition, reused.** Title, subtitle, hero line and first screenshot (or hook, blurb and tagline) must say the same thing at different lengths.
- **Write the short form first** (logline, tagline, one-sentence value prop), then expand and adapt per platform. Each platform gets its own version; never paste the same copy everywhere.
- **Produce variants, then recommend one** with a one-sentence rationale.
- **No unproven superlatives** ("#1", "best"). Ask first for regulated domains (health, finance, children). Do not use competitor trademarks.
- **Verify character limits** against current platform documentation before delivering; limits change.
