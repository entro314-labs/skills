# Award, competition and grant submissions

A scorecard document: every paragraph must earn points against a stated criterion. Judges compare entries, often score criterion by criterion, and may auto-disqualify missing sections or over-length entries.

## Process

1. **Gather the brief.** Award or grant name and organiser, selection criteria (verbatim, in order), word limits per criterion and total, weighting or judging rubric, category, first entry or resubmission, achievements and metrics the applicant wants included. Read published judging guides and past winner profiles if available; they show what evaluators value.
2. **Map achievements to criteria before writing.**

| Criterion | Key achievement | Evidence or metric | Story or example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innovation | [what changed] | [before to after, with units] | [one concrete case] |
| Growth | | | |
| Community | | | |

Every criterion needs at least one row. A blank row is a missing section. If evidence is missing, flag the gap to the applicant now instead of filling with vague text.
3. **Write in the form's exact criterion order**, one self-contained response per criterion (a judge reading only that section must understand it). Use STAR: Situation (1-2 sentences), Task, Action (concrete, what was done and how), Result (quantified).
4. **Review.** Word counts per section and total; every criterion addressed; every claim backed by evidence; first sentence of each section leads with impact.

## Writing rules

- **Lead with impact, not chronology.** Opening sentence is the strongest claim with a number ("Revenue grew 40% in 12 months while client retention held at 94%"). History is one context line, never the opener.
- **Quantify.** "Significant growth" becomes "revenue up 40% (850K to 1.19M)"; "many clients" becomes "127 active clients in 3 regions". If exact figures are unavailable, use defensible qualifiers: "approximately", "more than", "over the past 12 months". Never invent numbers.
- **Show, do not tell.** Replace adjectives (innovative, passionate, exceptional) with the evidence that implies them.
- **Use the word limit.** Stay within it (aim for about 95-100%); under-using leaves points unclaimed. To cut: remove context the judges already know, merge STAR elements, drop hedges ("we believe"), keep one strong example instead of three weak ones.
- **No superlatives without proof.** "Most innovative in the region" invites scepticism; the evidence should make the case.
- **Active, direct voice.** "Our approach cut processing time 60%", not "it is believed our approach may have contributed to improved outcomes".

## What each type looks for

| Type | Looks for |
|---|---|
| Business awards | Growth with numbers, innovation in products or operations, leadership and team development, community contribution, resilience |
| Industry or professional awards | Technical excellence and method, measurable client outcomes, thought leadership (publications, speaking, mentoring), contribution beyond own business |
| Grants | Clear problem statement, feasibility (can you deliver), budget with each line tied to an activity, measurable outcomes, alignment with the funder's stated objectives |

## Worked contrast (innovation criterion)

Vague: "We are committed to innovation and regularly explore new technologies."
Strong: "In Q1 we deployed automated content workflows across 40 client accounts, cutting production time from 5 days to 8 hours per campaign while client satisfaction held at 4.7/5 and costs fell 35%. All 12 staff completed a six-week internal training programme, and three clients have since adopted the method."
The strong version names the innovation, quantifies impact, explains implementation and shows downstream effect, in fewer words.

## Situational rules

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| First-time applicant | Win on specific evidence and a genuine story, not polish |
| Small entrant vs large | Do not match scale; use agility, per-capita impact and percentage growth |
| Resubmission | Address every point of feedback; strengthen the weakest section; add new achievements since: "Building on our earlier entry, we have since..." |
| Several categories | Tailor each entry; identical text across categories misaligns with different criteria and weightings |
| Criterion with no achievement | Acknowledge honestly and pivot to the closest real strength; do not pad |

## Anti-patterns

Vague claims without evidence; hyperbole; an unaddressed criterion; company-history dump; passive voice and hedging; unsupported awards or credentials; reusing one text for all categories.
