# Book Marketing Copy

Marketing copy promises an experience; it creates desire, it does not summarise. Test: "Why will readers regret not reading this?", not "What happens in this book?"

## Sequence

Logline first (25-35 words: protagonist + situation + stakes), then the blurb, then per-platform adaptations. Collect before writing: elemental genre, emotional core, stakes in one sentence, 3-5 comparable titles (from the last five years).

## Diagnosis (symptom -> check -> fix)

| State | Symptom | Check / fix |
|---|---|---|
| M1 No copy | Nothing exists | Identify genre, emotional core, one-sentence stakes, comps. Logline first, then blurb |
| M2 Summarises | "First X happens, then Y" | Opening sentence creates curiosity; protagonist introduced with emotional context; stakes in first 50 words; end on tension, not resolution. Replace events with emotional stakes; cut after the moment of choice; use "What will X do when..." |
| M3 Wrong genre signal | Romance reads literary; thriller sounds cozy | Compare tone, comp titles, genre keywords; promise must match delivery |
| M4 Generic | Adjectives instead of images | Add one specific detail to the hook; replace "dangerous" with what makes it dangerous; find the "only in this book" element |
| M5 Platform mismatch | Same copy everywhere | Retailer page uses limited formatting; back cover trimmed to physical limits; query structured for agents; social copy formatted for scrolling |
| M6 Tagline does not stick | Long or flat | Under 10 words, rhythm (read aloud), a question or promise, no filler; generate several, cut |

## Genre promise

| Genre | Copy must promise |
|---|---|
| Wonder | Awe, new perspectives |
| Horror | Fear, can't-look-away tension |
| Mystery | A puzzle, clues |
| Thriller | Stakes, countdown, survival |
| Romance | Emotional connection, satisfying resolution |
| Adventure | Excitement, what happens next |
| Drama | Character transformation |
| Humor | Laughter, wit |
| Idea | Perspective shift |

## Deliverables

1. **Back-of-book blurb, 150-200 words.** Hook 25-50 words (a character in a situation with a concrete detail); setup 50-75 (wants, obstacle, complication); stakes question 25-50 (what they risk, ends in tension). First sentence stops the browser; protagonist named in paragraph one; no spoilers past act one.
2. **Retailer description, 200-400 words, mobile-first.** Bold hook line, plain short paragraphs, italic stakes line, bold subhead, comp line ("Perfect for fans of [Comp 1] and [Comp 2]"). Use only bold, italic and paragraph breaks (limited HTML subset).
3. **Taglines, under 10 words.** Promise ("A love story that will break you and rebuild you"); question ("What would you sacrifice to save everyone you love?"); contrast ("She hunts monsters. She is one."); comparison ("[Comp] meets [Comp]").
4. **Query letter pitch, 150-200 words.** Hook (1-2 sentences: core conflict plus uniqueness); setup 50-75 words (name, identity, want, obstacle); stakes 50-75 words (escalation, personal loss, impossible choice); meta line: "[TITLE] is a [word count] [genre] novel, perfect for fans of [Comp 1] and [Comp 2]."

## Plot summary to desire

- Before: "Kira discovers she has magic."
- After: "Kira has spent sixteen years hiding her fire: from the authorities who would execute her, from the family who would disown her, even from herself."

## Anti-patterns

| Pattern | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Synopsis trap | first X, then Y, then Z |
| Vague intrigue | "nothing is as it seems" with no specifics |
| Spoiler reveal | describes the climax or resolution |
| Feature list | "magic, romance and adventure" with no experience |
| Throat clear | backstory before the hook |
| Wrong voice | literary copy for commercial fiction, or the reverse |
| Author intrusion | "I wrote this because..." |

Out of scope: writing the book, choosing the genre, author bios, website or paid-ad copy, promising sales.
