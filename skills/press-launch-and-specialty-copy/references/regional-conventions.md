# Regional Conventions

Orientation, not hard rules: send windows and agency styles vary by outlet. Wire services and distribution vendors are intentionally not listed; conventions only.

## Universal

- Send Tuesday-Thursday, mid-morning local time. Avoid Monday morning (inbox backlog), Friday afternoon, lunch, national holidays.
- Write the master release to the primary market's conventions, then localise (cultural adaptation, not translation). Use in-region professionals for review.
- Adapt quotes per market.
- Date formats: US Month Day, Year; UK/Oceania Day Month Year; France day month year; Germany Day. Month Year; East Asia Year Month Day.
- Metric units outside the US; local currency first, USD/EUR equivalent in parentheses.
- Simultaneous multi-region release: coordinate time zones. If staggered, lead with the market where the news matters most.
- Never send simplified Chinese to Taiwan or traditional to mainland China; never send Spanish to Brazilian media; Spain Spanish does not fit Latin America.

## Western Europe

- **France:** agency style, strict informational tone, no promotional language. Answer who, what, where, when, how and why in the lead. Use French quotation marks with non-breaking spaces. French often runs longer than English (commonly cited as 20-25%): adapt, do not translate. Avoid August.
- **Germany/DACH:** formal, verifiable claims, about 15 words per sentence. Avoid bridge days between a holiday and a weekend.
- **UK/Ireland:** British English, "Notes to Editors" section, understatement (journalists are sceptical of hype). Honorifics (Sir, Dame, Professor).
- **Spain:** formal. Avoid August and siesta hours.
- **Italy:** avoid August (Ferragosto) and Friday afternoon.
- **Benelux:** national agencies set style; Belgium often needs bilingual NL/FR. Avoid Carnival in the south.
- **Listed companies:** EU Market Abuse Regulation applies EU-wide; national regulators (AMF France, BaFin Germany, FCA UK, CNMV Spain, CONSOB Italy) and their disclosure channels apply.

## Nordics and Eastern Europe

- **Nordics:** lean writing; self-promotion reads badly, so let facts carry. Avoid midsummer week, July, Christmas-New Year (Norway: also Easter week).
- **Eastern Europe:** national wire agencies set style. Local language for domestic media, English for international. Formal, institution-focused. Avoid national holidays, Easter, Christmas, Midsummer (Baltics).

## Americas

- **US:** AP style. Avoid Monday morning and Friday afternoon; earnings outside trading hours. West Coast outlets: adjust to roughly 10 AM-2 PM PT.
- **Canada:** CP style, close to AP with Canadian English. Federal and regulated-industry releases often need simultaneous English and French. Listed companies file on SEDAR+.
- **Latin America:** Spanish for most markets, Portuguese for Brazil. Longer releases (400-600 words) accepted. National securities regulators (CNBV Mexico, CVM Brazil, CNV Argentina, CMF Chile, SFC Colombia). Avoid Dia de Muertos, Semana Santa, Carnival, year-end. Adapt Spanish per country.

## Middle East, Africa, Central Asia

- **GCC/Levant/Egypt:** national agencies set style. Arabic and English bilingual for business news; right-to-left layout. Honorifics (Sheikh, His Excellency, Dr.). Hijri dates may be required for government releases. Working week is Sunday-Thursday. Ramadan shortens hours; avoid Eid. Government communication offices gatekeep in several Gulf states.
- **Africa:** no single style. English in East/Southern business press, French in West/Central, Portuguese in Mozambique/Angola. South Africa: JSE continuous-disclosure rules and BBBEE context. Avoid December-January. Direct journalist outreach is the main channel.
- **Central Asia:** national agencies dominate; Russian-language media influence; government channels often mandatory for major announcements.

## Asia-Pacific

- **China:** pre-approval may apply; social platforms matter as much as wires. Avoid Chinese New Year (one to two weeks of media shutdown) and Golden Week (Oct 1-7).
- **Japan:** honorifics; press (kisha) clubs gate access, restricting direct pitching. Avoid Golden Week, Obon, Dec 28-Jan 3.
- **South Korea:** portal placement is key. Avoid Chuseok and Lunar New Year.
- **Taiwan:** traditional Chinese characters.
- **Southeast Asia:** English is the business language in Singapore, Philippines, Malaysia; local language for Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian media; bilingual releases common. Avoid Lunar New Year, Ramadan/Eid, Songkran, Tet, Holy Week.
- **South Asia:** English dominates business press; regional-language outlets matter in India. Direct pitching is the main channel. Avoid Diwali, Holi, national days, major cricket tournaments.
- **Australia/NZ:** closer to UK conventions than US; Australian spelling (colour, organisation, centre). Self-promotion is punished ("tall poppy"). Avoid Dec 20-Jan 15, Easter, ANZAC Day. Listed companies (ASX) release to the exchange first, then media.
