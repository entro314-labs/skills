# Pitching and Media-Format Adaptation

The pitch email is read before the release; a bad pitch means the release is never opened.

## Pitch rules

- Paste the full release in the email body; never attach it (unknown senders' attachments go unopened).
- 3 paragraphs maximum above the release, 3-5 sentences total.
- One journalist, one email. Never BCC a list. Research the journalist: read their last 3-5 pieces and reference one specifically. Never "Dear journalist".
- Pitch only what the journalist covers. A fintech reporter will not cover a restaurant launch.
- Exclusives: offer one only to one outlet. An "exclusive" sent to several burns every one of those relationships.

### Structure

1. **Hook** (1-2 sentences): the only part many will read.
2. **News summary** (2-3 sentences): what, plain language, the strongest data point, why this journalist should care.
3. **CTA** (1-2 sentences): a specific offer: CEO interview, early data, demo, exclusive briefing.

### Hook types

| Hook | Template | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Data | "[Surprising stat]. [Company] just released [thing] that addresses this." | Research, launches with metrics |
| Trend | "You have been covering [trend]. [Company] just [action] connected to it." | Funding, partnerships, market moves |
| Contrarian | "Everyone says [belief]. [Company]'s data shows the opposite." | Research, culture stories |
| Exclusive | "I would like to offer you an exclusive on [news] before we go public on [date]." | Major launches, funding, M&A |
| Local angle | "[Company]'s new [thing] will affect [local area] by [specific impact]." | Expansion, hiring, regional impact |
| Human interest | "[Person] spent [time] doing [thing]. Now they are [action tied to news]." | Founder stories, hires |
| Timeliness | "With [event/date] approaching, [Company] is [action]." | Seasonal, regulatory deadlines |
| Problem-solution | "[Audience] has long struggled with [problem]. [Company] just launched [solution] with [specific result]." | B2B tools, market entry |

### Subject lines

- Under 50 characters ideal, 60 maximum; the newsworthy element in the first 40 characters (phones truncate).
- No ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks, no "PRESS RELEASE:" prefix.
- Offer 2-3 variants. Patterns: data ("European SaaS churn drops 23% - new report"), exclusive, timely hook, genuine question, name-drop (the better-known partner first), local angle, contrarian, plain announcement ("[Company] launches [Product] - first [category] to [do X]").

### Follow-up cadence

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Initial pitch |
| 2-3 | Brief follow-up that adds an asset or offer |
| 5-7 | Final follow-up with a new angle or development |
| After 7 | Stop. Never a third follow-up |

Every follow-up must add value (new data, angle, or offer); never "just bumping this". If the journalist declines, thank them and move on. Track journalist, outlet, date sent, follow-ups, response.

## Media formats

### Print

- Wire/agency style per country; third person; past tense for events, present for ongoing situations.
- Images need typed captions and credits; clean plain text, no HTML; a separate factsheet/backgrounder; executive headshots at 300 dpi minimum.
- Lead times: national monthlies 4-6 months; weeklies 2-8 weeks; daily features 2-3 weeks; daily hard news 1-3 days (same day if breaking); Sunday supplements 4-6 weeks.

### Digital / wire

- Headline under about 100 characters (about 65 shows fully in search results); subheadline under about 160; body 300-500 words; end with `###` or `-30-`.
- Public companies: ticker on first reference, "[Company Name] (NYSE: TICK)".
- At most 3 links, descriptive anchor text (never "click here"), most important link in paragraph one; UTM parameters for tracking.
- Press-release links carry little direct link-building value; coverage links do.
- Make the first paragraph a standalone entity definition ("X is a [category] company that [does what]"), keep sections quotable on their own, add descriptive alt text. A short FAQ at the end (3-5 questions) helps answer engines and readers.

### Broadcast (write for the ear)

- Sentences 8-12 words; contractions always; active voice; attribution first ("The CEO said the company plans to...").
- Spell out abbreviations on first reference; round numbers ("nearly 50-thousand users"); present tense preferred; paraphrase instead of direct quotes in body copy.
- Speaking rate 150-160 words per minute: 30 seconds is about 75 words, 60 seconds about 150. Read aloud to time it.
- Soundbite: under 15 seconds spoken (about 35 words), self-contained, conversational.
- Provide a media advisory (who, where, when for interviews), suggested interview questions, B-roll with natural sound, mobile-friendly materials. Disclose the source of supplied video.

### Trade / industry

- Jargon is expected and signals expertise; lead with industry-specific impact, not general market trends.
- Narrow beats: research each outlet's exact scope. Include specifications, benchmarks, methodology, standards or certifications, and a specific comparison to existing solutions.
- Length can stretch to 500-700 words. Industry context is assumed.
- Tailored exclusives to one key outlet beat broad distribution. Bylined articles and named-customer case studies outperform releases for credibility.

### Social / modular release

- Components: core facts (bulleted, under 400 words), pre-approved standalone quotes, specific links (not generic "learn more"), embedded media, pre-written share copy.
- Most organisations use a hybrid: standard release structure plus pre-written social copy and embedded media.
- Professional networks: lead with the most surprising data point, framed through the spokesperson's perspective. Short-post platforms: 3-5 variants under the character limit, at most 2 hashtags. Visual platforms: carousel or short video, quote cards.

## Multimedia and press kit

- Images: JPEG or PNG; high-res (300 dpi print) and web versions; caption and credit on each; descriptive alt text ("Bar chart showing 15% YoY revenue growth for Q3 2025", not "chart.png"). Types: headshots, product shots, infographics, event photos, screenshots.
- Video: under 90 seconds for web/social; broadcast-quality and web versions; subtitles; transcript; 2-3 minutes of B-roll with natural sound.
- Infographics: key data only, branded, source cited inside the graphic, embedded and downloadable.
- Press kit: release (plain text + PDF); captioned images; executive bios and headshots; company factsheet (founded, HQ, headcount, key metrics); screenshots or demo video; FAQ; 2-3 top prior clips; logo files and brand guidelines.
