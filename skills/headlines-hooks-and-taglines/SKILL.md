# Headlines, Hooks, Taglines and Slogans

Two families of work with one shared method: generate many candidates, then select with a rubric rather than gut feel.

| Artifact | Job | Typical length | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline / title | Earn the next read, promise a specific payoff | 6-12 words | Page, ad, email subject, article |
| Hook | Stop the scroll; pull the reader into sentence 2 | 1-3 sentences, first one short | Social post, video/script opening, lead paragraph |
| Tagline / slogan | Public, repeatable brand expression | 2-7 words (ideal 3-5) | Logo lockup, ads, packaging, site header |
| Trueline / brand mantra | Internal compass for decisions | ~3 words | Internal only, never published |

## Core rules (in priority order)

1. **Volume first, then select.** Write at least 10 variations (taglines: 10-15 for slogans, 100+ raw for a brand tagline) before judging any. The first idea is rarely the best.
2. **Know the story before the line.** Reduce the content to Problem + Goal + Path. The headline or hook promises movement along that path. Extract the six elements: Problem, Goal, Benefit, Concept, Example, Process. Pair two of them (5-10 pairings, keep 2-3).
3. **Specific beats vague.** Replace "boost productivity" with "double output in 30 days"; "many people" with a count. Concrete numbers, named audiences, picturable outcomes. Never invent statistics: use placeholders or the user's real figures.
4. **Promise a clear benefit, not the process.** The reader asks "what is in it for me?". If the line explains how before it states why, flip it.
5. **Never promise what the content cannot deliver.** Curiosity gaps are fine; false claims are clickbait and burn trust.
6. **One structure per line.** Do not drift between formulas mid-headline.
7. **Confident wording.** Strip hedges (maybe, might, could, perhaps) unless the claim is genuinely uncertain.
8. **Plain words.** No jargon, no puns or literary allusions in headlines, no cleverness that blurs meaning. If you confuse, you lose.
9. **Select with a score, not a feeling.** 4 U's on the top 3-5; rubric on the finalists. See `references/scoring-and-selection.md`.
10. **Check as the reader**, before and after selection: What is in it for me? Too abstract to picture? Would I stop scrolling? Does it make sense standalone (no surrounding context)?

## Workflow

### Headlines and hooks
1. **Gather inputs**: topic or content, audience, desired action, channel and length limit, tone, any figures that are real and usable. If the audience or promise is unclear and it changes the outcome, ask.
2. **Ideate by questioning**: ask who/what/where/when/why/how about the topic, then ask "why" again on the promising answer, 2-3 levels deep. Stopping at the first answer is the most common ideation failure.
3. **Extract elements** (Problem, Goal, Benefit, Concept, Example, Process) and pair them. Pairings map to formulas (`references/headline-formulas.md`).
4. **Draft 10+ variations** across at least 3 formulas (headlines) or 3 hook types (hooks). Add one sticky device where natural: alliteration, symmetry, contrast, rhythm, rhyme (`references/hooks.md`).
5. **Filter** with the commandments checklist (aim for 4-6 hits per line, not all 10).
6. **Score** the top 3-5 with the 4 U's (pass 3/4), then rate finalists on the 5-part rubric.
7. **Deliver** the top 3 with the reason each won, plus 2-3 alternates in a different angle. State which assumption you made.

Time split when the headline sits inside larger content: about 25% ideation, 25% hook/headline, 50% body. The hook is not a quick afterthought.

### Hook-specific rules
- Social hook: max 3 sentences; first sentence short; each sentence must pull to the next.
- Pick exactly one hook type per hook (Curiosity, Transformation, Authority, Problem callout, Target callout, Bold claim). Templates in `references/hooks.md`.
- The body must pay off the hook in the first screen or first 10 seconds.

### Taglines and slogans
1. **Inputs**: product/brand name, one-sentence benefit, audience, brand personality, key differentiator, words or themes to include or avoid.
2. **Position first.** Write the trueline (internal compass), then the public tagline. Positioning precedes wording; a tagline cannot fix a vague position.
3. **Audit the field**: list competitor taglines; note shared words and clichés to avoid.
4. **Draft 10-15** across six angles, 2-3 each: benefit, emotional, wordplay, aspirational, problem-solution, brand promise.
5. **Distill** the USP: write it in full, cut the word count in half three times, then apply one device (rhyme, alliteration, parallelism).
6. **Score** each on six criteria (1-5): memorable, clear, differentiating, on-brand, versatile, timeless. Max 30. Recommend top 3.
7. **Test**: Onlyness test, ABC test, AIDA check, no-context reader test, translation and unintended-meaning check, small-format and large-format legibility. Details in `references/taglines-and-slogans.md`.

Audience awareness picks the approach: most aware -> direct product message; product-aware -> benefit; solution-aware -> differentiation; problem-aware -> problem then solution; unaware -> identity or aspiration.

## Length guidance and conflict resolution

- Headlines: 6-12 words is the working range (specificity needs room); email subjects and social hooks run shorter in the first line.
- Taglines: 2-7 words, ideal 3-5; never above 8. Short lines are easier to recall; stop trimming when clarity drops.
- Claims such as "most-recalled slogans average ~4 words" are rough research folklore: use as direction only, never cite as fact.
- "Use negativity/loss" (hook checklist) vs "avoid negative words" (classic headline rule): loss framing ("mistakes", "stop losing...") suits attention-limited hooks and is worth testing; for headline claims about the product itself, state the positive outcome. Test both when channel allows.
- The 4 U's, the 10 commandments and the 20/25 cutoff are practitioner heuristics, not validated thresholds. Use them to force discipline and compare options, not as proof.

## Common failures and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Surface-level ideation | Ask "why" 2-3 levels deep |
| Too abstract | Name the picturable outcome and number |
| Vague quantity | Replace with a count or range |
| Formula drift | Keep one structure per line |
| Hedge words | Delete them |
| Clickbait | Make the content deliver the promise or soften the line |
| Gut-based selection | Score with 4 U's and rubric |
| Too generic tagline | Onlyness test; if a competitor name fits, rewrite |
| Features, no benefit | Ask "so what?" until a benefit appears |
| Overly clever | Choose the direct version |
| Copying a famous line | Search existing slogans; confirm no competitor could use it |
| Translation blind spots | Test in each target language and market |
| No testing | A/B test headlines; panel-test taglines |

## Output format to use

Present candidates as a numbered list grouped by formula/angle, then a compact score table (columns: option, scores, total, pass/fail), then a ranked top 3 with one-line rationale each. Do not show raw brainstorm bulk unless asked; show the best 10-15.
