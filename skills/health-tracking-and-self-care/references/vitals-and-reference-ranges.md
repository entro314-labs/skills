# Vitals and Reference Ranges

Ranges are adult screening categories unless stated. Present as "typical range", never as a diagnosis. Confirm with repeat measurement and a clinician.

## Blood pressure (mmHg, seated, rested 5 min, arm at heart level)

| Category | Systolic | | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | < 120 | and | < 80 |
| Elevated | 120-129 | and | < 80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 130-139 | or | 80-89 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | >= 140 | or | >= 90 |
| Hypertensive crisis | > 180 | and/or | > 120 |

- Crisis with symptoms (chest pain, breathlessness, vision change, weakness, severe headache): emergency. Crisis without symptoms: recheck after 5 minutes of rest and contact a clinician the same day.
- Diagnosis rests on readings across separate occasions or home/ambulatory averages. Thresholds differ slightly between guideline bodies (some use 140/90 for stage 1); mention this if the user cites another number.
- Measurement errors that inflate readings: talking, full bladder, caffeine or smoking within 30 min, cuff over clothing, wrong cuff size, legs crossed, unsupported arm.

## Glucose and HbA1c

| Test | Normal | Prediabetes | Diabetes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fasting plasma glucose (>= 8 h) | < 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) | 100-125 (5.6-6.9) | >= 126 (7.0) |
| HbA1c | < 5.7% | 5.7-6.4% | >= 6.5% |
| 2 h oral glucose tolerance test | < 140 mg/dL | 140-199 | >= 200 |

- The 2 h cutoffs apply to a formal glucose tolerance test, not to a random post-meal finger-stick. A single post-meal home reading does not diagnose anything; use fasting and HbA1c as the user-facing reference and treat the OGTT row as lab context.
- Diagnosis normally requires confirmation on a second test unless there are clear symptoms with a very high value.
- Low glucose (< 70 mg/dL / 3.9 mmol/L) in a person on glucose-lowering medication: 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate, recheck in 15 min, repeat if still low; emergency help if confused, unable to swallow, or unresponsive.
- Exercise with diabetes: check before and after, carry fast carbohydrate, and if glucose is very high (a commonly used caution threshold is above about 250 mg/dL) check ketones and ask the care team before exercising.

## Heart rate

| Group | Resting rate (bpm) |
|---|---|
| Adults | 60-100 |
| Children | about 70-100 (varies by age; higher in infants) |
| Trained athletes | about 40-60 |

- Estimated maximum heart rate = 220 - age, with roughly +/- 10 bpm individual spread. Label as an estimate; beta blockers and other drugs lower achievable rates.
- Seek care for resting rate persistently above 100 or below 50 with symptoms (dizziness, fainting, chest discomfort, breathlessness), or an irregular, racing rhythm that does not settle.

## Oxygen saturation (SpO2, pulse oximeter)

- Normal about 95-100%. Below 95%: recheck (warm hands, still, no nail polish) and contact a clinician if it stays low. Below 90%, or any low reading with breathlessness: seek urgent care.
- Consumer oximeters can read inaccurately with cold hands, motion, nail polish, and on darker skin tones (tends to overestimate); treat a borderline reading with symptoms as more important than the number. Some chronic lung conditions have lower targets set by the clinician.

## BMI (kg/m2)

Underweight < 18.5; normal 18.5-24.9; overweight 25-29.9; obese >= 30.

- Screening tool only: ignores muscle mass, fat distribution, age, and ethnicity (some guidelines use lower cutoffs for people of Asian descent). Pair with waist circumference or clinical context before drawing conclusions.
- Not valid for children and teens (use age/sex percentiles) or pregnancy.

## Temperature (adult, oral)

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 36.1-37.2 C (97-99 F) | Typical |
| 37.3-38.0 C (99-100.3 F) | Low grade |
| >= 38.0 C (100.4 F) | Fever |
| > 39.4 C (103 F) | High fever, seek care |

Fever with rash that does not fade under pressure, stiff neck, confusion, breathing difficulty, or lasting more than 3 days in adults: seek care.

### Infants and children
| Age | Seek care |
|---|---|
| Under 3 months | Any temperature >= 38 C (100.4 F): same-day emergency evaluation |
| 3-6 months | Above 39 C, or fever persisting 48 h, or poor feeding/lethargy |
| Over 6 months | Fever with no improvement after 48 h, inability to drink, rash, unusual drowsiness, or a child who looks seriously unwell |

Do not give aspirin to children or teenagers with fever or viral illness (Reye syndrome risk). Dosing of paracetamol/acetaminophen or ibuprofen in children is weight-based: refer to the product label or pharmacist.

## Sleep (recommended hours per 24 h)

Behavioral sleep coaching is in the `sleep-and-burnout-recovery` skill.


0-3 mo 14-17; 4-12 mo 12-16; 1-2 y 11-14; 3-5 y 10-13; 6-12 y 9-12; teens 8-10; adults 18-64 7-9; adults 65+ 7-8. Persistent insomnia over 3 months, loud snoring with daytime sleepiness or witnessed pauses in breathing: recommend clinical assessment.

Suggested next step: {recheck under standard conditions / discuss at your next appointment / contact a clinician today / call emergency services if {symptoms}}."
