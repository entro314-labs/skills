# Travel Health

Rules differ by country and change; always tell the user to confirm with the destination's official health and customs authorities and their own clinician or travel-medicine clinic. Do not quote quantity limits or banned-substance lists as current fact.

## Timeline checklist

**4-8 weeks before (earlier for complex trips)**
- Travel-medicine or clinician visit: destination-specific vaccines (some need multiple doses over weeks), malaria prophylaxis if the area is endemic, altitude medication if needed, review of chronic conditions and fitness to fly.
- Travel and medical-evacuation insurance; check coverage for pre-existing conditions and activities.
- Confirm routine vaccines are current (tetanus-diphtheria, measles, seasonal influenza as appropriate).
- Dental and eye check if away for long.

**1-2 weeks before**
- Medicines: enough for the whole trip plus extra days for delays; original labelled packaging; prescription copies and a clinician letter listing diagnoses, generic drug names, and doses.
- Vaccination record, insurance details, emergency contacts, allergy and blood type card (see `profile-and-emergency-card.md`).
- Research local emergency number, nearest reliable hospital, and embassy contact.

**Day of travel**
- Carry medicines, insulin, and devices in hand luggage, never only in checked bags. Keep temperature-sensitive items cooled; ask the airline and airport security about needles, liquids, and devices in advance.

## Travel kit
Pain/fever relief, antihistamine, anti-diarrheal, oral rehydration salts, motion-sickness remedy, adhesive bandages, antiseptic wipes, antibiotic ointment, tweezers, thermometer, sunscreen, insect repellent (with a proven active ingredient such as DEET or picaridin), hand sanitizer, personal prescriptions.
Destination add-ons: water-purification method, bed net or permethrin-treated clothing, altitude medicine (prescription), malaria prophylaxis (prescription), extra sun protection, a first-aid kit for remote areas.

## In transit
- Move every 2-3 hours on long journeys; calf exercises, aisle walks; compression stockings for long flights if at higher clot risk (prior clots, recent surgery, pregnancy, estrogen therapy, obesity); stay hydrated; limit alcohol.
- Jet lag: shift sleep and light exposure gradually toward the destination schedule; time daylight and meals to the new zone; keep medicines on a schedule by interval, and ask the prescriber how to shift time-critical doses (insulin, anticoagulants, contraceptives).

## Food and water safety
- Drink sealed bottled or properly treated water (boiled 1 minute, filtered, or chemically treated); avoid ice of unknown source and unpeeled raw produce washed in tap water.
- Eat food that is freshly cooked and served hot; peel fruit yourself; be cautious with raw seafood, unpasteurized dairy, and buffets that sit out.
- Wash or sanitize hands before eating and after the toilet.
- Traveler's diarrhea: oral rehydration first; seek care for blood in stool, fever, severe pain, signs of dehydration, symptoms beyond 2-3 days, or in children, older adults, and pregnancy.

## Altitude
Above roughly 2,500 m, ascend gradually (sleeping altitude rises about 300-500 m per night above 3,000 m, with a rest day every 3-4 days), keep hydrated, avoid alcohol and heavy exertion early. Headache with nausea or fatigue is mild altitude illness: stop ascending and rest. Confusion, loss of coordination, breathlessness at rest, or a wet cough means descend and seek help urgently.

## Sun, heat, insects
Sunscreen SPF 30+ reapplied every 2 hours and after swimming, hat, shade at midday. Insect bites: cover skin at dawn/dusk in mosquito areas, repellent, nets. Fever after returning from a malaria-risk area is an emergency until proven otherwise.

## Carrying medicines across borders (principles only)
- Keep medicines in original packaging with the pharmacy label; carry a prescription and a letter from the prescriber.
- Quantities for personal use (often around a 1-3 month supply) are commonly accepted; larger amounts, injectables, and controlled substances may need advance permits.
- Ingredients commonly restricted somewhere include codeine and other opioids, pseudoephedrine/ephedrine, stimulants for ADHD, benzodiazepines, and cannabis-derived products. Some common over-the-counter products are prescription-only or banned abroad.
- Some traditional medicines contain protected-species ingredients (musk, tiger bone, rhino horn) that are illegal to move across borders.
- If unsure, check the destination's official customs and health regulator guidance and the embassy; declare rather than conceal; consider asking the prescriber for an alternative.

## Post-trip watch list
Seek care for fever (especially within weeks of return from a tropical area, and tell the clinician where you traveled), persistent diarrhea, rash, jaundice, unusual fatigue, cough lasting over 2 weeks, or any symptom lasting more than a week. Resume the regular medication schedule and check refills; complete any prophylaxis course after return as directed.
