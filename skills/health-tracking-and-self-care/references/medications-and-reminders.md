# Medications and Reminders

Role: help the user organize and remember their own prescribed regimen. Do not choose, change, or stop therapy.

## Medication list (what to capture per item)

Name (brand and generic), strength, form, dose, frequency and times, with or without food, purpose, prescriber, start date, refill date and quantity left, known side effects experienced, and whether it is as-needed. Include over-the-counter products, vitamins, and herbal supplements; they cause interactions too.

## Reminder schedule design

- Anchor doses to routines (breakfast, brushing teeth, bedtime) rather than clock times alone; use fixed clock times for medicines where spacing matters (for example every 8 or 12 hours).
- Consolidate to the fewest dosing times the prescriber allows; ask the pharmacist whether timing can be aligned. Do not move doses on your own.
- Reminder content: medicine name, dose, food instruction, and a confirm/snooze/skip action. Log each outcome (taken, late, skipped, reason) so the user can show the pattern to the prescriber.
- Refill reminders: trigger when about 7 days of supply remain; long-acting or critical medicines (insulin, anticoagulants, antiepileptics, heart medicines) earlier.
- Escalation: if a critical dose is repeatedly missed, prompt the user to contact the prescriber rather than auto-nagging.
- Missed dose: do not generalize. Say "check the leaflet or ask your pharmacist what to do for this medicine"; never advise doubling up.
- Pill organizers (weekly, with AM/PM compartments) help for stable regimens; keep medicines in original packaging when traveling.

## Adherence barriers to ask about
Side effects, cost, complex schedule, forgetting, feeling better and stopping, fear of dependence, unclear purpose. Address the barrier and encourage a conversation with the prescriber instead of silently stopping.

## Common drug classes (general information only)

| Condition | Typical classes (examples) |
|---|---|
| Hypertension | ACE inhibitors (enalapril, lisinopril), ARBs (losartan, valsartan), beta blockers (metoprolol), thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide) |
| Type 2 diabetes | Metformin, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors, insulin |
| High cholesterol | Statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin), fibrates, ezetimibe |
| Minor pain/fever | Acetaminophen (paracetamol), ibuprofen, naproxen |

State the class and general purpose only; indication, dose, and choice belong to the prescriber.

## Interaction flags to surface (ask the pharmacist to confirm)

| Combination | Concern |
|---|---|
| Warfarin + aspirin (or other NSAIDs) | Increased bleeding |
| Metformin + heavy alcohol | Risk of lactic acidosis |
| Some statins (notably simvastatin, atorvastatin) + grapefruit | Higher statin levels, muscle toxicity risk |
| ACE inhibitor/ARB + potassium supplements or salt substitutes | High potassium |
| NSAIDs + ACE inhibitor/ARB + diuretic | Kidney injury risk |
| Multiple sedating products (alcohol, sleep aids, opioids, some antihistamines) | Excess sedation, breathing depression |
| Two products containing the same ingredient (for example several cold remedies with paracetamol) | Accidental overdose |

This list is non-exhaustive: absence of a flag is not a clearance. Always end with "check with your pharmacist or prescriber before combining".

## Supplements

- Say how strong the evidence is (strong: several randomized trials or meta-analyses; moderate: some trials, mixed; limited or preclinical: early data) and name the study type. If evidence is weak or the product is unfamiliar, say so; never invent study counts, scores or interaction data, and answer "based on general knowledge" when no reference was checked.
- Introduce one supplement at a time, about 2 weeks apart, so benefit or side effects can be attributed. For a "does it work for me" test, record a baseline (sleep, energy, symptoms), dose, form and timing, review after the trial period, and keep stopped products with the reason.
- Record per product: name and form, dose, timing, start date; keep the medication list and supplement list together so both are checked.
- Re-check the supplement list when the user mentions a new prescription, a diagnosis, a changed or stopped medicine, pregnancy or trying to conceive, or upcoming surgery. Trigger only on those, not on every health mention ("I have a headache" is not a trigger; "taking ibuprofen daily" is).
- Report by severity: high (lead with it and give the specific action), moderate (mention with a practical step such as monitoring or timing), none (one line). Well-founded examples: St John's wort lowers the effect of many drugs (including oral contraceptives) and can cause serotonin toxicity with antidepressants; several botanicals are avoided in pregnancy; supplements that affect bleeding or sedation should be disclosed before surgery.
- Stay calm and specific, do not repeat a flag already given in the conversation, and do not override the prescriber: the interaction may already be accounted for, so end with "confirm with your prescriber or pharmacist".

### Supplement timing and absorption (general; product label and pharmacist override)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fat-soluble products with a meal containing fat | Vitamins A, D, E, K, omega-3 oils, CoQ10, curcumin extracts; absorption is poor on an empty stomach |
| Iron | Better tolerated with food if the stomach is upset; vitamin C aids absorption; keep about 2 h apart from calcium, dairy, coffee and tea |
| Large mineral doses compete | Calcium, magnesium, zinc and iron at high doses reduce each other's uptake; split large doses. Long-term high zinc can deplete copper |
| Separate from certain drugs by 2-4 h | Magnesium, calcium, iron, zinc and antacids with tetracycline or fluoroquinolone antibiotics, bisphosphonates and levothyroxine (levothyroxine: about 4 h from calcium and iron); confirm the gap with the pharmacist |
| Stimulating or sleep-affecting products | Commonly taken in the morning: B-complex, caffeine-containing products; evening: magnesium glycinate, glycine, L-theanine (all small-evidence aids) |
| Consistency beats timing | A schedule the user can keep daily is better than an optimal one that is skipped; tie it to meals |

Combinations to flag for the prescriber or pharmacist: omega-3, curcumin, vitamin E or ginkgo with anticoagulants or antiplatelets (bleeding; also disclose before surgery); vitamin K products with warfarin (keep intake constant, do not start without the prescriber); CoQ10 reported to weaken warfarin; high-dose vitamin D or calcium with thiazide diuretics (high calcium); long-term high-dose vitamin B6 (nerve damage); magnesium or potassium products in kidney disease.

## Safe-use rules to repeat
- Follow the label and prescriber instructions; complete antibiotic courses as prescribed.
- Do not share prescriptions; do not use leftover medicines for a new illness without advice.
- Store away from heat, damp, and children; check expiry dates; dispose via pharmacy take-back where available.
- Allergy or serious reaction (swelling, trouble breathing, widespread rash): stop and seek emergency care.
- Pregnancy, breastfeeding, kidney or liver disease, and age under 12 or over 65 change safety of many drugs: refer to a professional.
