# First Aid Basics

Scope: what a layperson can do until professional help arrives. Recommend a recognized first-aid course for hands-on skills. Always begin with: scene safe? call local emergency number (put on speaker, keep the caller with the patient); then act.

## Priority order
1. Scene safety. 2. Responsive? 3. Breathing normally? 4. Life-threatening bleeding? 5. Call/delegate the call. 6. Treat the most serious problem first. 7. Keep the person warm and still; do not give food or drink to someone who may need surgery or has reduced consciousness.

## CPR (adult, unresponsive and not breathing normally)
1. Check response (tap shoulders, shout). Call emergency services and send someone for an automated external defibrillator (AED).
2. Place the person on their back on a firm surface.
3. Hand centre of the chest (lower half of breastbone). Push hard and fast: about 5-6 cm (2 in) deep, 100-120 compressions per minute, allow full recoil, minimize pauses.
4. Untrained or unwilling to give breaths: compression-only CPR is acceptable. Trained: 30 compressions then 2 rescue breaths, repeat.
5. Use the AED as soon as it arrives; follow its voice prompts. Continue until help takes over, the person responds, or you are too exhausted.
- Children and infants: ideally begin with 5 rescue breaths (or 2 per local protocol) when cause is likely respiratory; compress about one third of chest depth; use one hand (child) or two fingers (infant). Use pediatric AED pads if available. Encourage a trained course for infant/child CPR.

## Choking
| Person | Steps (conscious, cannot cough, speak or breathe) |
|---|---|
| Adult / child over 1 | Encourage coughing if effective. If not: 5 back blows between shoulder blades, then 5 abdominal thrusts; alternate until clear. If they become unresponsive, call emergency services and start CPR. |
| Infant under 1 | Face-down along forearm, head lower than chest: 5 back blows; then face-up: 5 chest compressions (two fingers); repeat. No abdominal thrusts. |
Anyone who needed abdominal thrusts should be medically checked afterward.

## Severe bleeding
1. Apply firm direct pressure with a clean cloth or dressing; keep pressing.
2. Do not remove a soaked dressing; add layers on top.
3. Raise the injured limb if no fracture is suspected; lay the person down.
4. Life-threatening limb bleeding not controlled by direct pressure: apply a commercial tourniquet 5-8 cm above the wound (not over a joint), tighten until bleeding stops, note the time, do not loosen.
5. Call emergency services. Treat for shock (lie flat, legs raised if no injury prevents it, keep warm).
Nosebleed: sit up, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose 10-15 min. Seek care if it will not stop or follows a head injury.

## Heart attack and stroke
- **Heart attack signs:** chest pressure or pain, spreading to arm, jaw, neck or back, sweating, nausea, breathlessness; women and older adults may have fatigue, nausea, or back/jaw pain without classic chest pain. Call emergency services immediately, sit the person in a comfortable position, loosen tight clothing. Aspirin may be advised by emergency staff or local first-aid guidance (commonly a single chewed adult tablet) if not allergic and not otherwise told to avoid it; follow the dispatcher. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR and use an AED.
- **Stroke (FAST):** Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech slurred or confused, Time to call emergency services. Note when symptoms started. Do not give food, drink, or medication. Other signs: sudden vision loss, severe sudden headache, loss of balance.

## Burns
| Degree | Look | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Superficial (1st) | Red, painful, no blisters | Cool under running water for 20 min (10-20 min is the minimum commonly taught), then loose clean cover |
| Partial (2nd) | Blisters, very painful | Cool the same way; do not pop blisters; cover loosely; seek medical care, especially if larger than the person's palm, or on face, hands, feet, genitals, or over a joint |
| Full thickness (3rd) | White, leathery, charred, may be numb | Emergency; cover loosely with clean non-fluffy material; do not cool large areas for long (hypothermia risk) |
- No ice, butter, toothpaste or oil. Remove rings and watches near the burn before swelling. Chemical burn: brush off dry powder, flush with plenty of water 20+ min. Electrical burns, inhalation of smoke, and burns in children or older adults: emergency evaluation.

## Sprains and strains
Rest and protect the joint, apply cold for about 15-20 min at a time with a cloth barrier, compression with an elastic bandage (not tight enough to cause numbness or color change), elevate. Rest is now balanced with gradual early movement as pain allows. Seek care if the person cannot bear weight, has severe swelling, deformity, numbness, or pain over bone.

## Allergic reactions
- **Mild** (hives, itching, runny nose): avoid trigger, antihistamine per label, cool compress.
- **Severe or anaphylaxis** (breathing difficulty, wheeze, swelling of face/lips/throat, faintness, widespread hives with vomiting after exposure): give epinephrine auto-injector immediately into the outer thigh if available (can be repeated after 5-15 min per device instructions), call emergency services, lay the person flat with legs raised (sit up if breathing is difficult; lie on side if vomiting or unconscious). Antihistamines do not treat anaphylaxis and must not replace epinephrine. The person needs hospital evaluation even if symptoms resolve.

## Common minor conditions

| Problem | Self-care | Seek care if |
|---|---|---|
| Dehydration (diarrhea, vomiting, heat) | Frequent small sips of oral rehydration solution; continue feeding infants | Cannot keep fluids down, no urination 8+ h, sunken eyes, extreme drowsiness, blood in stool, infants/older adults early |
| Upset stomach | Clear fluids, bland foods as tolerated (a strict BRAT diet is not required), avoid fatty food; handwashing | Blood in vomit or stool, severe or localized abdominal pain, fever with persistent vomiting, symptoms beyond 2 days |
| Tension headache | Rest, hydration, regular meals, gentle neck stretches, simple analgesic per label | Worst-ever or thunderclap headache, fever with stiff neck, after head injury, with weakness/speech/vision change, new headache over age 50 |
| Migraine | Quiet dark room, cold pack, early analgesic per label, anti-nausea if prescribed | Pattern change, aura with weakness, first-ever severe attack |
| Heat exhaustion | Move to cool place, remove excess clothing, sip fluids, cool skin | Confusion, no sweating, temperature over 40 C (heat stroke: emergency, cool aggressively while waiting) |
| Minor cut or scrape | Wash with clean water, pressure to stop bleeding, cover | Deep, gaping, dirty, animal/human bite, no tetanus booster in 10 years |
| Head injury | Rest, observe | Loss of consciousness, vomiting, drowsiness, confusion, clear fluid from nose/ears, seizure: emergency |
| Poisoning/overdose | Call poison control or emergency services at once; have the container and amount ready; do not induce vomiting | Any suspected overdose, even if the person seems fine |

## Kit contents (home)
Adhesive bandages, sterile gauze and tape, elastic bandage, triangular bandage, antiseptic wipes, antibiotic ointment, tweezers, scissors, thermometer, disposable gloves, CPR face shield, oral rehydration salts, antihistamine, simple analgesic, saline eye wash, instant cold pack, emergency blanket. Check expiry dates twice a year; list emergency numbers inside the lid.
