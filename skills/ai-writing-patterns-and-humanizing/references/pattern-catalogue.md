# Pattern catalogue

Each entry: the tell, then the fix. Examples are structural illustrations.

## Content and significance

| Pattern | Tell | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Significance inflation | stands/serves as, testament, pivotal moment, key turning point, evolving landscape, indelible mark, setting the stage for, marking/shaping the, represents a shift | State what happened. If the sentence works without the inflation clause, delete the clause |
| Notability name-dropping | "cited in several major newspapers and broadcasters" | One specific reference with context: "In a 2024 interview, she argued..." |
| Superficial -ing analysis | trailing participles: highlighting, underscoring, emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting, symbolizing, contributing to, fostering, encompassing, showcasing | Replace with a fact, attribute the reason to a named person if one exists, or cut |
| Promotional language | nestled, breathtaking, vibrant, rich cultural heritage, stunning natural beauty, must-visit, in the heart of | Plain description ("is a town in the X region"). If you would not say it in conversation, cut it |
| Vague attribution | "Experts believe", "Studies show", "Industry reports", "Observers have noted", "Research suggests" | Name the source, or state the claim and drop the attribution. Never invent a citation. If the author has real but uncontrolled experience, say that |
| Formulaic challenges | "Despite challenges, X continues to thrive" | Name the concrete challenge and a dated, specific response, or cut |
| False concession | "While X is impressive, Y remains a challenge" | Make both halves specific or take a side |
| False ranges | "from the Big Bang to dark matter" | List the real topics |
| Formulaic opening | "In the rapidly evolving world of...", "In today's [X]", "In an era where" | Lead with the news or insight; context second |
| Generic conclusion | "The future looks bright", "Exciting times lie ahead", "As we move forward" | Cut, or end on a specific next step ("The company plans to open two more locations next year.") |

Before and after (significance inflation):
- Before: "The institute was established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics."
- After: "The institute was established in 1989 to collect and publish regional statistics independently of the national office."

Before and after (-ing analysis): "The palette was chosen, symbolizing local heritage" becomes "The architect said the palette references local bluebonnets." (only if the source exists).

## Syntax and grammar

| Pattern | Tell | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Copula avoidance | serves as, stands as, represents, features, boasts, offers, presents | is / has. "The gallery serves as the exhibition space. It features four spaces and boasts 3,000 sq ft." becomes "The gallery is the exhibition space. It has four rooms totaling 3,000 sq ft." |
| Negative parallelism | "It's not just about X; it's Y", "not merely a song, it's a statement", em-dash form "isn't X, it's Y" | State the positive claim once. Max one per piece, only when it serves the argument |
| Compulsive rule of three | triplets everywhere; "adjective, adjective, and adjective" | Vary group size (two, four, a full sentence). Drop a third item that only completes the trio. "Keynotes, panels, and networking" becomes "talks and panels, plus time for informal networking" |
| Synonym cycling | protagonist, main character, central figure, hero in one paragraph | Repeat the clearest word. Same term for the same concept. If the right noun appears three times, keep all three |
| Gerund fragment lists | "X is the main challenge. Fixing bugs. Writing code." | Fold into one sentence with a colon |
| Hedging | typically, generally, potentially, possibly, perhaps, somewhat, "could possibly", "it could be argued", "to be clear"; "might" where "may" or "will" is meant | Test: if the hedge protects a claim that should be stated, cut it. If the uncertainty is real, make it specific ("I don't have data for this, but based on..."). Do not swap in new hedges |
| Rhetorical-question openers | "But what does this mean for developers?", "So why should you care?" | State the answer. One question that points at a real tension is acceptable; a question used as a section transition is a tell |
| Parenthetical hedging | "(and, increasingly, Z)" | Own sentence or cut |
| Let's-constructions | "Let's dive in", "Let's break this down" | Start with the point. Keep "let's" only as a real invitation to act |
| Acknowledgment loops | "You're asking about...", "To answer your question..." | Answer directly |
| Reasoning-chain residue | "Step 1:", "Breaking this down", "Working through this logically", "Let me think step by step" | State the conclusion, then the evidence |
| Confidence-calibration adverbs | Interestingly, Importantly, Notably, Surprisingly, Undoubtedly | Let the fact speak; flag by density |
| Emotional flatline | "What surprised me most", "I was fascinated to discover", "The most interesting part" | If the content is surprising, show it. Claim an emotion only if the surrounding writing earns it. Applies to human writing too |
| Transitions | Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition | Restructure or use "and", "also", "but", "so", "because" |

## Formatting

| Pattern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Em dashes | Target zero. Cap one per 1,000 words by default (profiles in `profiles-and-triage.md`). Replace with comma, period, colon, parentheses, "and". Apply to headings too. Catch `--` |
| Bold overuse | At most one bolded phrase per major section, or none. Restructure to lead with the point |
| Inline-header bullets | `- **Performance:** Performance improved...` becomes a prose sentence. Items that need headers are probably paragraphs |
| Title Case Headings | Sentence case for subheadings; title case only for the main title |
| Emoji in headers/bullets | Remove. Social posts may keep one or two at the end of a line |
| Numbered list inflation | "Five things to know": number only discrete parallel items |
| Excessive structure | More than 3 headings in under 300 words; 8+ bullets in under 200 words |
| Formulaic headers | Overview, Key Points, Summary, Conclusion, Introduction: name what the section contains |
| Bullet addiction | Convert to prose when items are under ten words, form a sequence, or sit in prose context |
| Five-paragraph template | Definition-first opener, three parallel body sections, restatement closer: start where the interest starts, end when the point is made |
| Hyper-symmetrical paragraphs/sections | Same length and rhythm throughout: vary; a one-sentence paragraph is a tool |
| Curly quotes | Weak signal; normalize only if house style requires |

## Chatbot and conversational residue (remove entirely)

- Openers and closers: "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you need anything else", "Feel free to reach out", "Certainly!", "Of course!", "Absolutely!", "Great question!", "Here is an overview of...", "Let's dive in!".
- Sycophancy: "You're absolutely right!", "That's an excellent point". It validates the reader instead of informing them.
- Cutoff and access disclaimers: "As of my last update", "While specific details are limited based on available information", "I don't have access to real-time data". Find the information or remove the hedge. Never publish a sentence that admits the writer did not look something up.
- Template residue: `[Your Name]`, `[Insert X here]`, "As an AI", "Based on my training". Search for these before publishing.
- Opening restatement of the prompt; meta-narration ("In this article, we will explore...", "First, I will... Second, I will...").
- False warmth: "We hope this guide has been helpful on your journey", "exciting developments", "incredible opportunity".

## Content-level tells that wording cannot fix

- Specificity gap: 5+ vague claims with no data or attribution. Flag to the user and ask for proof. Do not fill the gap.
- Mostly-pattern piece: rewrite rather than patch.
- No voice context: ask for one sample rather than guessing.
- Tone mismatch with the surrounding body of work: flag it.
