# Positioning & Messaging

Positioning determines WHAT makes you different. Messaging translates that into WHAT you say. Voice determines HOW you say it (see `brand-voice`). This skill covers the first two, plus the brand narrative that makes them memorable.

**When NOT to use:** deciding what to build (strategy problem), visual identity work, or pure copy polish with no positioning change.

## Inputs (ask for what's missing; proceed with labeled assumptions if unavailable)

- Product: what it is + what it does (1–3 sentences)
- ICP/persona and primary use case / job-to-be-done
- **The real alternative**: status quo, competitor, internal build, manual workaround — what would they do instead?
- Differentiators + proof (features, results, quotes, credibility signals)
- Primary surfaces: website hero, sales pitch, deck, press
- Constraints: tone, compliance, taboo words

Never invent facts. Unsupported claims get labeled "to validate" with an evidence-to-collect list.

## Workflow

### 1. Choose the "against" alternative + category frame
Identify what you're actually displacing in the buyer's decision (usually the status quo, not a competitor). Pick a category frame the audience understands without a glossary — "«category» for «audience»" pattern-matching works especially well for press.

### 2. Write the positioning brief
Template: *For [target audience] who [need/pain], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [key differentiator].*

Expand into a brief: value, differentiation, proof, and explicit tradeoffs/non-goals (what you *don't* do). **Test:** the positioning must not plausibly fit 3 different competitors.

### 3. Build the messaging hierarchy (listener-first)
```
Core message (one sentence a first-time reader can restate)
├── Pillar 1 → proof points → objections answered
├── Pillar 2 → proof points → objections answered
└── Pillar 3 → proof points → objections answered
```
Add "what we mean / what we don't mean" notes per pillar to prevent internal drift.

### 4. Generate copy primitives
- **One-liner** and **elevator pitch** (30s)
- **Value proposition**: *[Product] helps [customer] [achieve outcome] by [differentiating mechanism] so they can [deeper benefit].* — e.g. "Loom helps remote teams communicate faster by replacing long email threads with instant screen recordings so they can move projects forward without another meeting."
- **Tagline options** (3–5) and **homepage hero headline/subhead** candidates
- Value prop checklist: customer + JTBD clear? Outcome specific and measurable? Mechanism distinguishes you? Matches real VoC language?

### 5. Craft the brand narrative (when story is needed)
For brand story, founder story, origin story, or pitch narrative:

- **Pick the story type**: brand / founder / origin / customer.
- **Find the five-second moment** — the singular instant of realization or transformation. It must be describable in 1–2 sentences and meaningfully different from "we had an idea." (Usually: they lost something, were cornered into bravery, or finally committed.)
- **Arc**: context → tension → moment → new belief → why it matters to the listener. Spend most of the time building context for the moment.
- **Intention & obstacle** at every beat: what does the hero want, what's in the way, what happens if they fail.
- **Low stakes beat high stakes for likability** — relatable small moments build more connection than dramatic extremes.
- **Scripts in three lengths**: 2-minute talk track, 30-second version, website About paragraph. Each must read aloud naturally.
- Attach proof to every major claim; add identity hooks ("this is who you become") for consumer brands — without manipulation.

Techniques and examples: `references/storytelling-techniques.md`

### 6. Consistency enablement
Produce a "say this / not that" table, a short internal description script, and a sales talk track (including a "reset" explanation for confused prospects). **Test:** two different team members would describe the product the same way.

### 7. Validation plan
Lightweight test with 5–8 questions across three dimensions: comprehension ("what is it?"), relevance ("is this for you?"), recall ("what do you remember a day later?"). Define a keep/revise decision rule up front.

## Deliverable: Positioning & Messaging Pack

1. Context snapshot (ICP, use case, surfaces, constraints, assumptions)
2. Positioning brief (category + against + differentiation + proof + tradeoffs)
3. Messaging hierarchy (core message + pillars + proof + objections)
4. Copy set (one-liner, pitch, taglines, hero options, CTA suggestions)
5. Brand narrative + scripts (when requested)
6. Consistency enablement (say-this/not-that, talk tracks)
7. Validation plan
8. **Risks / open questions / next steps** (always)

## Quality Gates

- A stakeholder can answer: "Who is this for, and what will this messaging change?"
- The "against" alternative is explicit
- A first-time reader can restate the value in one sentence
- Copy is concrete (specific nouns/verbs), no vague superlatives
- No unsupported claims; unknowns labeled
- Story is truthful — refuse requests to fabricate drama or numbers; offer placeholders + evidence-to-collect instead
