# Landing and Website Page Copy

Two modes. Build: produce section-by-section copy for a page. Audit: the user shares a URL or pasted copy; review in conversion-impact order and return fixes plus experiments. Detect the mode from what the user supplies.

## 1. Intake before writing

Minimum brief (at least 3 of 4 present or inferable, otherwise state assumptions and proceed rather than interrogating):

1. Product or offer in one line (name, price, guarantee, bonuses if any)
2. Target audience (role, situation, sophistication)
3. The single page goal and CTA (signup, trial, demo, purchase, lead, waitlist)
4. Evidence assets (named results, testimonials, user counts, logos, press, credentials)

Also establish: awareness level of the visitor, traffic source (ad copy, email, organic, referral), top 2-3 objections, top 2-3 pain points, and top 3 competitors/alternatives. Ask 2-3 critical questions at a time, not a long list.

## 2. Core rules (in order of importance)

1. **One page, one goal.** Every element supports the single primary CTA or is removed. Two goals means two pages. Landing pages get logo plus at most 0-3 nav links plus one CTA; footer carries privacy, terms, contact only, with no competing CTA or newsletter box.
2. **Match the page to the visitor.** Awareness level and traffic source decide the lead, the length and the CTA (section 3; `references/awareness-and-traffic-fit.md`). Ad promise appears immediately and the headline echoes the ad copy.
3. **Evidence decides the layout.** Never fabricate or round proof. No evidence means a different page type, not invented proof (section 5).
4. **Five-second test.** A cold visitor must grasp: what is this (clarity), is it for me (relevance), why care (value), what do I do next (action).
5. **Outcome over feature, specific over abstract, "you" over "we".** Lead with what changes for the reader; put the feature in support. Use customer language, not category jargon.
6. **Place proof next to the claim and next to the CTA.** Handle each objection where it arises, then again before the final CTA.
7. **Urgency only when real.** Real deadline, real cap, real seasonal relevance. Never reset timers or invented scarcity.
8. **Honest structure.** Section sequence matters more than any single section's polish; get the order right, then refine wording.

## 3. Fit the page to awareness level and traffic

| Visitor state | Lead with | Page shape |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | The symptom or a story they recognise, not the product | Long: problem, education, mechanism, then offer |
| Problem-aware | Pain acknowledgment and agitation | Educate on the solution category before introducing the product |
| Solution-aware | Differentiation: why this solution over the alternatives | Comparison, mechanism, proof |
| Product-aware / warm | Offer, proof, price, guarantee | Short; straight to proof, objections and CTA |

Price and commitment adjust length: impulse purchase (low price) gets a short hero-first page; mid price needs education plus extended features; high ticket (roughly 500+ or a sales-led service) needs long-form with heavy proof. These are heuristics, not measured thresholds. Cold paid traffic needs more explanation and message match; email and referral traffic from warm audiences needs less.

Details, frameworks (PAS, AIDA, BAB, 4Ps, customer-as-hero script) and the B2B sales-page sequence: `references/awareness-and-traffic-fit.md`.

## 4. Page structure

Default spine: Hero, social proof bar, problem, solution/benefits, how it works, features, testimonials, pricing or offer, FAQ, final CTA. Minimum viable page: hero + proof + CTA. Pick the order from the page type; do not include sections you cannot support.

| Page type | Core sections (in order) |
|---|---|
| SaaS tool | Hero, social proof, problem, solution, how it works, features, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, final CTA |
| Digital product | Hero, problem, solution, what's inside, testimonials, pricing, guarantee, FAQ, final CTA |
| Service / consulting | Hero, problem, solution, how it works, testimonials, founder/team, pricing or scope, FAQ, final CTA |
| Waitlist (no evidence) | Hero with email field, 1-2 sentence problem, at most 3 solution bullets, timeline, minimal footer |
| Demo / booking | Outcome headline with booking CTA, credibility bar, 3-4 step process, scope, testimonials, FAQ (3-5), booking CTA |
| Direct purchase | Hero with buy CTA, proof bar, problem, solution, how it works, features, pricing, risk reversal, FAQ (5-8), final CTA |
| Product page (e-commerce) | Buybox, proof row, UGC, benefits, specs, video/before-after, comparison, FAQ, final CTA |
| Free tool / OSS | Hero, how it works, features, demo, community proof, getting started |
| Newsletter / community | Hero with email capture, what you get, sample content, social proof, about the creator |

Full per-type orders, comparison and affiliate pages, e-commerce flow and word counts: `references/page-types-and-sections.md`.

Section sizing: problem 50-150 words; feature block 20-40; testimonial 20-50; FAQ answer 30-60; final CTA 15-30. How it works is always three steps, with the last one an outcome ("Sign up, Connect, Done"). Benefits: 3-6 with short title plus 1-2 sentences, outcome first. Feature grids in 3, 4 or 6 (not 5 or 7).

## 5. Evidence tiers (decide the layout)

| Tier | You have | Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Outcome proof | Real case studies, named results, user counts | Full testimonial section, exact numbers, pricing, proof bar |
| 2 Mechanism proof | Founder expertise, process, no customer cases | Founder story in place of testimonials, "why this works", methodology, no implied customer results |
| 3 Preview | Nothing yet | Waitlist layout only; no pricing, testimonials or proof bar; "beta"/"early access" framing |

If proof is missing, omit the section. Do not claim customer results even implicitly. Mark any example testimonial in a template as an example.

## 6. Hero and headline

Hero formula: headline (outcome) + subheadline (how, who, or the biggest objection) + primary CTA + optional secondary text link + visual of the real product in use or the real outcome. Add one proof line in or directly under the hero. Visible without scrolling on mobile, CTA included.

- Headline 6-12 words; subheadline 15-25 words. Specific, benefit-led, speaks to "you", no more than one idea.
- Formulas: "[Outcome] without [pain]"; "From [current state] to [desired state] in [time]"; "[Number] [things] to [outcome]"; "Stop [pain]. Start [benefit]."; "The [category] that [differentiator]"; "[Who] + [specific outcome] + [qualifier]"; verb-led benefit ("Ship faster"). Question hooks only when the pain is obvious.
- Subheadline formulas: method ("[Product type] that helps [audience] [promise]"), mechanism ("Using [method], [product] [benefit]"), contrast ("[Get benefit]. Without [sacrifice].").
- Screenshot test: would the reader screenshot it and send it to a colleague? Logo-swap test: if a competitor's logo fits and the page still reads correctly, it is generic.
- Avoid: "Welcome to...", "The world's most advanced AI-powered solution", "We help businesses grow", "Unlock your potential", logo sliders in the hero, autoplay video, hidden price, generic stock (handshakes, abstract geometry).

Weak to strong: "Our software solution enables organizations to optimize their workflow processes" becomes "Finish work in half the time". "We have helped numerous clients achieve their business objectives" becomes "500+ teams ship faster with us" (only if true). More patterns: `references/hero-headlines-ctas.md`.

## 7. CTA

- Formula: action verb + value (+ risk reducer). Describe the outcome of the click, not the form action.
- Good: "Start free trial", "See it in action", "Get the template", "Create your first report", "See pricing". Weak: "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more", "Sign up", "Download", "Contact us".
- First person often tests better ("Start my free trial"); treat as a test, not a law.
- Friction reducer directly under the button: "No credit card required", "Cancel anytime", "Setup takes 2 minutes".
- One primary CTA, same text throughout; at most one secondary as a text link. Repeat after each major section (hero, after features, after proof, final).
- CTA role by position: hero = direct action; after features = reinforce ("See it in action"); after proof = convert; final = recap plus urgency or reassurance.
- Final CTA block: restate value from a new angle, answer the last objection, same button text as hero, risk-reversal line.

## 8. Proof and trust

Strength order: specific results with numbers > named testimonial with photo > user count > client logos > star ratings > press mentions > generic praise (never use). B2B and high-ticket: named testimonials with title and company. Consumer: rating plus review count.

- Testimonial formula: "[Specific result]. [How it happened]." with full name, title, company. Good: "We cut onboarding from 3 weeks to 2 days. The templates saved us." Bad: "Great product, highly recommend!" by "John D."
- Placement: one proof line in the hero; logo bar right under it; dedicated testimonial block after benefits (2-3 per block, more than 5 fatigues); a stat or quote next to every CTA; trust badges near forms and pricing.
- Logo bars: consistent height, grayscale or high contrast, only recognisable brands.
- Press, guarantees, certifications and security details support trust but rank below customer outcomes. See `references/about-and-press-pages.md`.

## 9. Objection handling

Distribute, do not dump into the FAQ: biggest objection in the subheadline or hero support line; 2-3 handled indirectly inside benefits; risk reversal beside the final CTA; remaining objections explicit in the FAQ (real questions, most common first).

| Objection | Answer with |
|---|---|
| Too expensive | ROI, free tier, cost of not acting |
| No time to learn | Setup time ("2 minutes"), onboarding |
| What if it does not work | Guarantee, trial, similar-customer case study |
| Already using X | Comparison table, migration support |
| Is my data safe | Security and compliance detail, badges |
| Will I be locked in | "Cancel anytime", export |
| Is this legit | Testimonials, company info, founder story |
| Need approval | "Share with your manager" asset |

Guarantee copy states the mechanism (refund, trial), conditions if any, and for products shipping and secure checkout. FAQ length: 3-5 on demo pages, 5-8 on purchase pages.

## 10. Voice and cleanup

Second person, active voice, present tense, sentences under 20 words (ideally under 12), paragraphs 3-4 sentences max, scannable. Tone by audience: B2B enterprise confident and evidence-led; SaaS clear and low-friction; consumer friendly and benefit-driven; services trustworthy and relationship-focused. Default for solo-founder products: professional-casual.

Delete AI-sounding filler (revolutionize, cutting-edge, seamless, leverage, unlock, empower, game-changing, "in today's fast-paced world") and replace abstract verbs with number, outcome and timeframe: "Revolutionize your workflow" becomes "Cut reporting time from 2 hours to 10 minutes". Structural tells and fix formula: `references/copy-quality-and-anti-patterns.md`; vocabulary lists: `copywriting-ai-writing-patterns-and-humanizing`. Read the page aloud; if it sounds like a press release from any company, rewrite.

## 11. Compliance floor

Email capture needs a privacy link to a real URL. Payment needs terms plus refund or cancellation policy. EU audience needs cookie consent before deployment. Affiliate content discloses the relationship before the first affiliate link. No fake testimonials, reviews or counts; no confirmshaming ("No thanks, I don't care about saving money" becomes "No thanks").

## 12. Deliverables

When building: (1) copy document organised by section, (2) page code only if requested (semantic HTML, mobile-first), (3) conversion checklist. Include title (50-60 chars) and meta description (150-160 chars). When auditing: use the output order in `references/audit-and-testing.md`.
