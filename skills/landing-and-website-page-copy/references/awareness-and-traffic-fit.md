# Awareness level, traffic source and framework choice

## Awareness levels (what the visitor already knows)

| Level | Visitor thinks | Lead | Spend words on | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Has a symptom or none | Story, symptom, surprising fact | Naming the problem, education | Longest |
| Problem-aware | "I have this problem" | Pain acknowledgment | Problem, agitation, then solution category before product | Long |
| Solution-aware | "I know solutions exist" | Differentiation | Why this solution; tactful comparison to alternatives; mechanism | Medium |
| Product-aware | "I know you; am I convinced?" | Offer, deal | Proof, objections, guarantee | Short, fast to CTA |
| Most aware | Ready, needs a nudge | The offer and terms | Price, bonus, deadline (if real) | Shortest |

Unaware and most-aware are the extremes of the problem-to-product scale; most cold traffic from ads is problem- or solution-aware, most email and retargeting traffic is product-aware.

## Traffic-source fit

| Source | Do |
|---|---|
| Paid search | Headline echoes the query or ad promise; first screen answers it; strip nav |
| Paid social | Visitor was interrupted: earn attention with the ad's hook, repeat the creative's promise and imagery, lower-commitment CTA |
| Email to subscribers | Short; continue the email's thread; offer first |
| Organic / SEO | Answer the search intent first, then convert; more education, internal links allowed |
| Referral / partner | Mention the referrer's context; borrowed trust only if the referrer is real |
| Competitor-keyword traffic | Comparison page, factual and balanced |

Message match rule: the ad's promise appears on the page immediately, and the headline echoes the ad copy. A mismatch is the first thing to check when paid traffic bounces.

## Audience psychology inputs

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Current state | What frustrations do they face daily? |
| Desired state | What does success look like to them? |
| Hidden fears | What are they secretly afraid of? |
| Trust barriers | Why might they not believe you? |
| Decision triggers | What would make them act now? |

Use the answers for the problem section (emotional resonance), headline choice (awareness fit), objections addressed before they arise, and urgency that is authentic.

## Copy frameworks

| Framework | Use when | Mapping to the page |
|---|---|---|
| PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solve) | Specific, pain-driven product; default | Headline names pain; subheadline agitates; CTA offers the fix. "You're spending 20 hours a week on invoicing instead of building your product." |
| AIDA | Product needing awareness | Hero = attention; problem and benefits = interest; features and testimonials = desire; CTA sections = action |
| BAB (Before, After, Bridge) | Transformation with a vivid before/after (productivity, design, learning) | Before = current pain; After = outcome; Bridge = product |
| 4Ps (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push) | Clear proof points | Promise = headline; Picture = outcome visual; Proof = results; Push = CTA |
| Customer-as-hero script | Narrative or brand-led pages | Visitor is the hero, product is the guide; plan in three steps; success and failure stakes (`copywriting-brand-voice-and-storytelling`) |

Pick one as the spine; do not blend all five. The page structure is the main lever, the framework only orders the argument.

## Length by price and commitment (heuristics)

| Offer | Page |
|---|---|
| Impulse purchase, low price | Short, hero-first |
| Mid price | Education plus extended features, hero-first |
| High ticket or B2B service | Long-form with heavy proof |
| Pre-launch | Waitlist or launch sequence |
| Video-led offer | Video sales page plus order form |
| Free offer only | Squeeze page: headline, benefit bullets, email field |

## B2B sales page: psychological section sequence

Section order carries the persuasion; each section sets up the next. Skipping one breaks the chain.

| # | Section | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | Value proposition in 3 seconds |
| 2 | Trust strip | Specific numbers, not vague claims |
| 3 | Emotional reframe | Remove self-blame ("it is not your fault"); creates safety |
| 4 | Problem mirror | "This is exactly me" recognition |
| 5 | Consequences | Cost of inaction, make not buying costly |
| 6 | False solutions | Disqualify what they already tried |
| 7 | Core differentiator | Why your approach is structurally different |
| 8 | Outcomes | Concrete before/after |
| 9 | Scope / implementation areas | What is included (optional split from 8) |
| 10 | Process | Three-step simplicity |
| 11 | Testimonials | Logos plus quotes with results |
| 12 | ROI and decision pressure | Cost-per-day of inaction; real figures only |
| 13 | FAQ | The 5-7 real objections |
| 14 | Final CTA | Single clear action (steps 13 and 14 may be counted as one close) |

Tone: high-conviction, no hedging. "Your margins are bleeding. Here is why." beats "We might be able to help you improve...". CTA at least three times. Mobile must preserve the same flow.

False-alternative disqualification (before presenting your solution): "You've probably tried hiring more people (margins got worse), adding tools (complexity increased), piecemeal automation (new problems). None address the root cause..." Use only alternatives the audience really tries, and only claims you can defend.

Anti-patterns: polishing visuals before the sequence is right; a generic hero ("We help businesses grow" vs "More margin. More capacity. Without hiring."); skipping the problem mirror (Hero to Solution to CTA); an FAQ with three generic questions instead of the real 5-7 blockers.

## Paid-traffic landing page checklist

1. Ad promise visible immediately; headline echoes the ad.
2. Single goal and CTA; navigation removed or minimal.
3. Lead capture of 5 fields or fewer.
4. Proof close to the first CTA.
5. Page is linked from somewhere (no orphan) and links to privacy.
6. CTA reachable without scrolling on mobile.

## Five-step conversion flow (any new page)

1. Hook with the visitor's situation or desired outcome.
2. Show you understand (problem).
3. Show the way (solution and mechanism).
4. Prove it (evidence).
5. Remove risk and ask (offer, guarantee, CTA).
