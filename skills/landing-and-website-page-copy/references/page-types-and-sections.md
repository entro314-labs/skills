# Page types and section orders

Count sections against the page-type template before writing. Drop sections that lack real content.

## Landing page

| Type | Sections, in order |
|---|---|
| Waitlist (pre-launch, no evidence) | Nav with "Join waitlist" CTA; hero with teaser headline and one inline email field; problem teaser (1-2 sentences); solution preview (max 3 bullets); timeline ("Launching in Q2"); social proof only if real; minimal footer |
| Demo / booking | Nav with book-a-demo CTA; outcome headline with booking CTA; credibility bar (logos, client count, years); process of 3-4 steps; scope (in and out); testimonials if real; FAQ of 3-5 ("What happens after the demo?", "How long is the call?"); final booking CTA; footer |
| Direct purchase | Nav with buy CTA; benefit headline with buy CTA; proof bar; problem; solution; how it works; features (if 3+); pricing with what is included (required); risk reversal (required: guarantee, trial or refund); FAQ of 5-8 purchase objections; testimonials if available; final CTA; footer with privacy, terms, contact |
| Service lead gen | Nav with "Get in touch" / "Request a quote"; outcome headline; credibility (founder story, years, notable clients); methodology as Discovery, Proposal, Engagement, Delivery; scope with optional "not included" column; case studies and testimonials if available; FAQ of 5 service objections ("How long does an engagement take?", "What if it is not a good fit?"); contact form (name, email, message) or booking link |

Excluded from waitlist pages: pricing, FAQ beyond 2-3 items, feature grid, testimonials, full how-it-works, case study, comparison table. Reassurance line: "We'll only email you once, when we launch."

Product-type mapping:

| Product | Sections |
|---|---|
| SaaS tool | Hero, social proof, problem, solution, how it works, features, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, final CTA |
| Digital product (course, ebook, template) | Hero, problem, solution, what's inside, testimonials, pricing, guarantee, FAQ, final CTA |
| Service (consulting, coaching) | Hero, problem, solution, how it works, testimonials, founder/team, pricing, FAQ, final CTA |
| Physical product | Hero with product photo, benefits, how it works, testimonials, specs, pricing, guarantee, FAQ |
| Free tool / open source | Hero, how it works, features, demo or screenshot, community proof, getting started |
| Newsletter / community | Hero with email capture, what you'll get, sample content, social proof, about the creator |

Paid-traffic page types and where the CTA goes:

| Type | CTA destination / note |
|---|---|
| Click-through (warm audience) | Pricing, product or signup |
| Lead capture | Email form, 5 fields or fewer |
| Product-focused | Feature deep-dive then signup |
| Comparison (X vs Y) | For competitor brand keywords; leads to trial or pricing |
| Use-case / solutions | Segment-specific message, same single goal |
| Free tool (gated) | Tool access in exchange for email |
| Bridge / bonus | Extra incentive for buying through your link |
| Webinar / event registration | Registration form |

## Navigation and footer

Landing page nav: logo, 0-3 links, one CTA; sticky; on mobile collapse to logo plus CTA or a hamburger. Footer: privacy, terms, contact. Link the page from somewhere (nav, sitemap or campaign hub) so it is not orphaned, and link out to home, about and privacy.

Hero layouts: split (text left, visual right, most common), centered, full-width image with overlay, video background (only if not autoplay-with-sound and not the sole carrier of the message).

## Homepage (startup / product)

Hero; social proof bar (logos, numbers or a quote); problem; solution or how it works (3 steps or key features); benefits (outcome-focused); proof deep dive (testimonials, case studies, metrics); pricing preview or CTA section; FAQ. A homepage serves several audiences, so the hero names the category and outcome, and secondary navigation routes segments. Positioning statement to settle first: "For [target audience] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternatives], we [differentiator]."

## Comparison ("A vs B") page

Balanced template: hero; intro (context on both options, why the comparison matters); comparison table with factor rows (include price and rating rows where relevant); "When to choose A" and "When to choose B" lists of 3 reasons each; verdict with stated criteria; CTA. Per-product sections carry description, pros and cons, CTA. FAQ of 4-6 comparison questions. Avoid one-sided verdicts; a visibly biased comparison loses trust. Use dual CTA for the top two options when the page is independent.

## Affiliate single-product page

Disclosure above the fold before the first affiliate link; hero with headline, subtitle, primary CTA and trust signal; trust bar (rating, user count, press); 3-6 feature columns; price plus CTA; one strong testimonial; "who this is for" list; FAQ of 4-6 objections; final CTA; footer. At least 3 CTAs: hero, mid-page, bottom.

## E-commerce product page (default order, not a rule)

1. Nav
2. Buybox: image left; star rating and review count above the product name; name; price; variant selectors; primary "Add to cart"; secondary "Buy now"
3. Social proof row: 3 testimonial cards with stars and avatars
4. UGC or more proof (customer photos)
5. Benefits: 4-6 icon items
6. Ingredients or specs grid with labelled images, plus full spec table
7. Video or case study with before/after and a named customer
8. Comparison table versus two alternatives, including price and rating rows
9. FAQ accordion, 4-8 items
10. Final CTA: urgency headline only if real, large button, trust badges (guarantee, shipping, secure checkout), simplified footer

## Long-form sales page (high ticket or B2B service)

See the section sequence in `awareness-and-traffic-fit.md`. Offer stack section: what is included (with values only if genuine), price (anchor only if the anchor is real), bonuses, guarantee displayed prominently.

## Thin-page rule (site pages)

A standalone page needs about 5 or more sections and about 500 words of substance. If thinner, merge with another page, add real sections, or do not create it. Do not repeat the same information across pages.

## Section pattern for site pages

Every section: headline (2-6 words), one-sentence subheadline giving context, then body. Put key facts in the first 50 words. Mention pricing when applicable; end with a CTA.
