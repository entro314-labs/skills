# Proof, objections, risk reversal, urgency

## Proof hierarchy

1. Specific results with numbers (named customer, measurable outcome)
2. Named testimonial with photo, title, company
3. User or customer count
4. Client logos
5. Star ratings and review volume
6. Press mentions (see about-and-press-pages.md)
7. Generic praise: never use

B2B and high ticket: named testimonials strongest. Consumer: rating plus review count strong. Mechanism proof (founder expertise, process) substitutes when customer proof does not exist.

## Writing testimonials

Formula: "[Specific result or transformation]. [How it happened]." Attribution: full name, title, company (photo if available). A strong pattern: "[Result with numbers]. [What changed]. [Recommendation]."

- Good: "We cut onboarding from 3 weeks to 2 days. The templates saved us." Jane Ortiz, Head of Ops, Acme.
- Bad: "Great product! Love it!" by "John D."
- Length 20-50 words. 2-3 per block; more than five causes fatigue. Mix formats (quote, case-study snippet, video) rather than stacking identical cards.
- Stat format: "[Big number]+ [thing]", for example "4.9 stars from 2,000+ reviews".
- Real counts only. Never round up.

## Placement map

| Location | Proof |
|---|---|
| Hero | One strong line (rating, count, or single quote) |
| Directly below hero | Logo bar or stat strip |
| After benefits | Dedicated testimonial or case-study section |
| Next to every CTA | One stat or short quote |
| Near forms, pricing, checkout | Trust badges (security, guarantee, payment) |
| FAQ and final CTA | Risk reversal and the most relevant testimonial |

Put proof immediately after the claim it supports; proof in a far-away section does not carry the claim.

## Logo bars

Consistent height, grayscale or high contrast with hover color, only recognisable brands, not a slider in the hero. If logos are unknown, use a count ("Used by 1,200 finance teams") or omit.

## When proof is missing

- Omit the section; do not invent quotes, counts or logos.
- Use "beta", "early access" or "built by someone with 10 years in X" framing.
- Replace testimonials with founder story and "why this works" mechanism.
- Switch to a waitlist layout (evidence tier 3) rather than faking a sales page.

## Objection handling map

Place each objection where it arises:

| Where | What to handle |
|---|---|
| Hero subheadline | The single biggest objection |
| Benefits section | 2-3 objections indirectly (speed, ease, cost) |
| Pricing and CTA area | Risk reversal |
| FAQ | Remaining objections, explicit, real questions, most common first |
| Before final CTA | Re-state guarantee and the strongest proof |

Standard objections and answers:

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| Too expensive | ROI calculation, free tier, cost of not using |
| No time to learn | Setup time, onboarding, templates |
| What if it does not work | Money-back guarantee, free trial, similar-customer case studies |
| Already using X | Comparison table, migration support, import tool |
| Is my data safe | Security badges, compliance, encryption details |
| Will I get locked in | "Cancel anytime, no questions asked", data export |
| Is this legit | Testimonials, company information, founder story |
| What if I need help | "Free support included", response time |
| Need approval | "Share with your manager" page, one-pager |

## Risk reversal

Money-back guarantee, free trial, free first month, no credit card, cancel anytime, free support. State the mechanism and any conditions in plain words. Product pages add shipping and secure-checkout reassurance. Put it beside the final CTA and the pricing, not only in the footer.

FAQ rules: 5-8 questions on purchase pages, 3-5 on demo pages, 4-6 on comparison pages; answers 30-60 words; write in the customer's words; lead with the biggest objection; cut manufactured questions. FAQ content can be marked up as structured data when it reflects real visible content.

## Urgency

Allowed only if real:

- Launch pricing with an end date
- Cohort enrollment with real deadlines
- Genuinely limited seats or stock
- Seasonal relevance

Never: reset countdown timers, "Only 3 left" on unlimited supply, "limited time" on evergreen offers, fabricated user counts, waitlist positions without a real queue. Without real urgency, use reassurance and a recap instead ("Join 5,000 teams. Cancel anytime."). Where the cost of inaction is real, express it in a number (cost per week of the problem), not in pressure language.

## Evidence-based layout switch

| Evidence | Page |
|---|---|
| Real case studies, named results | Full sales page with testimonials and exact numbers |
| Founder expertise, process | Founder-led page, methodology, no implied customer results |
| Nothing | Waitlist preview; explain it can be upgraded once evidence exists |
