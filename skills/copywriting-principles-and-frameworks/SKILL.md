# Copywriting principles and frameworks

Copy that converts is clear, specific, in the customer's own words, and built around one action. Research and structure decide the result more than wordsmithing.

## Workflow

1. **Collect inputs (ask only for what is missing, otherwise state assumptions and proceed).**
   - Page type and purpose; the ONE primary action (and any secondary one).
   - Ideal customer, their problem, what they already tried, top objections.
   - Offer, differentiation, key transformation, real proof points.
   - Traffic source and what the visitor saw/knows before this page.
   - Brand voice guide if one exists (it overrides general voice rules).
   - Optional brief-lock mode: if the user asks for a formal brief, summarise goal, audience, value proposition, CTA, awareness level and assumptions, and wait for confirmation. Do not require this for every request.
2. **Research customer language** before drafting (see `references/research-voc.md`). Never invent customer language, statistics, testimonials or guarantees; where proof is missing, insert an explicit placeholder such as `[PROOF NEEDED: metric]`.
3. **Set the awareness level** of the reader, then pick a framework (see tables below).
4. **Draft headline first**: write many variants, pick 2-3 finalists (`references/headlines-and-ctas.md`).
5. **Structure the page** by page type, one argument per section (`references/page-structures.md`).
6. **Edit** with the sweeps in `copywriting-copy-editing-and-review`, then score (`references/editing-and-scoring.md`).
7. **Deliver** copy grouped by section (headline, subheadline, CTA, section heads, body, secondary CTAs), with 2-3 alternatives for headlines and CTAs, a one-line principle annotation on each key choice, and page title + meta description when relevant. Optionally tag confidence: strong / test this / needs proof.

## Core rules (ordered by importance)

1. **Clarity beats cleverness.** If clear and creative conflict, choose clear. One job per sentence, one idea per section. Puns and humour only when clarity is free.
2. **Specific beats vague.** "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" beats "Save time on your workflow". Exact numbers read as credible, but only real ones.
3. **Customer's words, not company jargon.** Mirror phrases from reviews, tickets, calls and forums.
4. **Reader-first.** Lead with the reader's problem or outcome, not "We..." or the company name. Keep "you" well ahead of "we" (aim at least 3:1).
5. **Features -> benefit -> outcome.** Bridge with "which means...". Always write at the "so what" level.
6. **Copy channels existing desire; it does not create it.** Find the desire the reader already has and intensify it.
7. **One primary CTA** per page/section, repeated with the same action; match it to awareness.
8. **Proof over claims.** Show, don't tell. Every claim needs evidence nearby.
9. **Preempt objections** before the final CTA; do not wait for them.
10. **Honesty.** Real urgency only, real guarantees only, no fabricated proof, no manufactured "early signals".
11. **Be direct and confident.** Lead with value, not qualifications; cut "very", "really", "almost"; no exclamation points.
12. **Read aloud.** If it sounds awkward or like nobody would say it, rewrite.

## Awareness level decides the lead

Most copy fails by targeting the wrong level. Less aware means longer copy.

| Level | Lead with | Page job |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Story, pattern interrupt, a question naming a symptom | Make the problem visible |
| Problem-aware | Empathy + "a better way exists" | Name the solution category |
| Solution-aware | Why YOUR solution (differentiation, mechanism) | Differentiate with proof |
| Product-aware | Why NOW: proof, offer, risk reversal | Overcome last objections |
| Most-aware | Price, offer, deadline, CTA | Remove final friction |

## Framework selection

| Situation | Framework |
|---|---|
| Pain-driven offer, warm audience, email, short ad, retargeting | PAS (problem, agitate cost of inaction, solve) |
| Landing page, ad, long email, product launch | AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action) |
| Case study, testimonial, transformation | BAB (before, after, bridge) |
| Ad under ~100 words, short landing page | 4Ps (promise, picture, proof, push) |
| Product description, comparison | FAB (feature, advantage, benefit) |
| Cold audience, long page, webinar, VSL | PASTOR (problem, amplify, story, testimony/transformation, offer, response) |
| Nurture email sequence, brand narrative | Star-Story-Solution |
| Hooks, about sections, case snippets | Micro-story (50-100 words: person with problem, failed common fix, different approach, specific result) |

Direct-response backbone for any long copy: hook, problem validation, mechanism ("most people try X; what works is Y"), solution, proof, objection handling ("you might be thinking X; here is the truth"), CTA, real urgency. Details and templates: `references/frameworks.md`.

## Headlines (most effort goes here)

- One core message, specific, front-loaded. Roughly 6-12 words; under about 10 above the fold on landing pages; ~60 characters or less for search-result titles.
- Strong categories: benefit, news, curiosity, ease of achievement. Self-interest first.
- Avoid: puns nobody gets, questions the reader would answer "no" to (a rhetorical question is fine only when "yes" is the expected answer), >12 words for ads/emails, leading with the company name.
- Match the headline to the ad/link that brought the visitor.
- Formulas, scoring and the volume-then-select workflow: `copywriting-headlines-hooks-and-taglines`. Subheadline and CTA rules: `references/headlines-and-ctas.md`.

## CTAs

- Formula: action verb + what they get + qualifier. "Start my free trial", "Get the complete checklist", "See pricing for my team".
- Avoid Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Click Here, Get Started, Send, Buy Now. First person ("my") often beats second ("your"); test it.
- Put friction removal in or beside the button ("No credit card required", "Takes 2 minutes").
- Above the fold, after value is explained (not before), after each major section on long pages, after objection handling. Always the same action.
- Match awareness: "See pricing" for ready buyers; "Learn more" only for the unaware.

## Page structure essentials

- Above the fold: headline, 1-2 sentence subheadline adding specificity, one primary CTA, one trust signal. Landing pages from ads: remove navigation.
- A feature-only stack (hero, features 1-4, CTA) is the weak pattern: it reads as a list, not an argument. Each section carries one argument and ends with a reason to continue.
- Page-type focus: homepage serves several audiences without going generic and leads with the broadest value; landing page = one message, one CTA; pricing page = recommended plan obvious, answers "which is right for me"; feature page = feature, benefit, outcome, use cases; about page = why you exist, tied to customer benefit, still has a CTA.
- Section orders by page type: `copywriting-landing-and-website-page-copy`. Enterprise, launch, long-form sales, VSL and pricing blueprints: `references/page-structures.md`.

## Proof, objections, urgency

- Proof strength (best first): named customer result with metric; specific metric; independent industry statistic; named testimonial with role; volume ("2,400+ teams"); logos/awards; star ratings; generic praise (discard). Match proof to the prospect's industry and size. Spread proof through the page, near every CTA.
- Objection map: too expensive -> cost of inaction / ROI math; won't work for me -> same-industry case study; no time -> time-to-value; already have one -> make switching cheap; what if it fails -> risk reversal; need to ask my boss -> shareable business case; later -> ongoing cost of delay.
- Urgency and scarcity only when real (dated deadline, stated capacity, cohort, early-bird with true end, cost of delay). Fake countdowns erode trust once the deadline passes.
- Guarantees: state exact terms and the outcome covered; promise only what the business will honour.
- Details, psychology levers, price anchoring: `references/proof-objections-and-persuasion.md`.

## Editing (always run before delivery)

Run the seven sweeps from `copywriting-copy-editing-and-review` (clarity, voice, so what, prove it, specificity, emotion, zero risk) in order. Then check:

- Jargon outsiders won't know; sentences with >3 conjunctions; passive voice; exclamation points; buzzwords with no substance.
- AI tells (stock openers, filler transitions, em dashes as default connector): `copywriting-ai-writing-patterns-and-humanizing`.
- Mobile: paragraphs max ~3 lines, CTA visible without scrolling, tap targets at least 44x44 px, noticeably less copy than desktop.
- Score with the 100-point rubric or the 8-question health check in `references/editing-and-scoring.md`; ship at 85+.

## Voice

Fix formality and personality before drafting (casual / professional-friendly / formal; playful or serious; bold or understated; technical or accessible). Keep voice consistent but vary intensity: headlines bolder, body clearer, CTAs action-oriented. Map internal terms to the customer's public terms.

## Proactive flags (raise without being asked)

Copy opens with "We" or the company name; vague value prop ("the best platform for teams"); features without benefits; no social proof; weak CTA verbs; multiple competing CTAs; wall of text; claims without evidence; fake urgency; ignoring mobile; never testing.

## Testing

One variable at a time. Highest-impact order: headline; CTA text/placement; proof type/placement; price framing; page length; media; colour/design. State a hypothesis and primary metric, pre-set duration, do not stop early. Sample-size math: `copywriting-conversion-optimization-and-page-audits`. A 5-second test checks clarity, not persuasion. See `references/testing.md`.
