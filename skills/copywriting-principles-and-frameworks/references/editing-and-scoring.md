# Style rules and scoring

The seven-sweep editing method, plain-English substitutions, readability metrics and review reports are in `copywriting-copy-editing-and-review`. AI-sounding wording and transitions are in `copywriting-ai-writing-patterns-and-humanizing`. This file holds the style rules and the page-level scoring used when writing.

## Style rules

- Simple over complex ("use", not "utilize").
- Specific over vague (cut "streamline", "optimize", "innovative", "leverage").
- Active voice.
- Confident over hedged (cut "very", "really", "almost"). Hedging words (may, might, could, tends to, often, typically, evidence suggests) belong only on genuinely uncertain claims.
- Show the outcome instead of adverbs like "instantly".
- Honest over sensational. No exclamation points.
- Max 3 conjunctions per sentence.
- Be direct: "Need to share a screenshot? Send as many files as you like." beats "X lets you share files instantly...".
- Three-pass shortcut: meaning (read as if you know nothing), flow (read aloud), trim 20-30%.

## Readability targets

| Medium | Grade level | Words per sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Social | ~5th | 8-12 |
| Email | 6-8th | 10-15 |
| Landing page | 7-8th | 12-18 |
| Blog | 8-9th | 15-20 |
| Technical docs | 10-12th | 18-25 |

Heuristics, not standards.

## Voice definition

Personality in 3 adjectives; we-are / we-are-not with examples; tone sliders (formal-casual, serious-playful, technical-simple, reserved-enthusiastic); always-use and never-use vocabulary; internal-term to public-term mapping; punctuation rules. A brand voice guide overrides general rules. Full method: `copywriting-brand-voice-and-storytelling`.

## Analytical and research long-form

Register for essays, research summaries and white papers (objective, accessible, not persuasion copy):

- Objective tone: state claims directly, avoid first-person and direct address; keep hedges only where uncertainty is real ("the evidence is inconclusive", "this is the most optimistic case"). Replace "some say" or "it could be argued" by stating the counterargument and answering it.
- Evidence before conclusion, and make it checkable: name people, companies, dates and numbers instead of "some researchers" or "often". Useful evidence types: dated historical cases, specific metrics, a pattern across several cases, a grounded thought experiment.
- Structures: puzzle -> investigation -> resolution; framework -> application -> implications; claim -> evidence -> counterargument -> refined claim.
- Paragraphs: one idea each, 3-6 sentences, up to about 10 for layered arguments; section headers on long pieces; a table or data block to break dense prose. Varied sentence length with a short sentence for the key claim; guiding questions ("Why there, and why then?") engage without addressing the reader.
- Edit loop: draft fast, rewrite for clarity and concision, read aloud, delete any sentence whose removal loses nothing, cut introductions and open on the interesting part. Replace vague words by a question they leave open ("important" -> why?).

## Claim discipline

Every claim must be testable or sourced. Never fabricate statistics, testimonials or guarantees. Missing proof gets an explicit placeholder. Treat example figures in any template as illustrative, never reuse them as real claims.

## Scoring: 100-point page rubric (final scoring)

Rate each dimension 0-5, multiply by weight, sum. Weights are hand-set; use the score to compare drafts, not to predict performance.

| Dimension | Weight | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | x4 | 20 |
| Clarity | x3 | 15 |
| Persuasion | x3 | 15 |
| Proof | x3 | 15 |
| CTA | x3 | 15 |
| Voice | x2 | 10 |
| Objection handling | x2 | 10 |

Grades: 85+ ship; 70-84 minor tweaks; 55-69 significant rewrite; 40-54 structural problems; below 40 start over with research.

Other scales for existing pages (A-D grades, eight-factor audit, weighted copy score) are in `copywriting-copy-editing-and-review` and `copywriting-conversion-optimization-and-page-audits`; pick one scale per report.

## Quick triage (40 points, first pass only)

Rate 1-5: clarity, specificity, desire, proof, urgency (real), friction, voice, CTA. Below 24 rewrite; below 32 significant opportunity.

## Copy health check (8 yes/no)

Headline passes "so what?"; top objection addressed; readable by a 12-year-old; one CTA per section; benefit over feature; would stop scrolling; every sentence earns its place; sounds human. Fewer than 6 yes: rewrite.

## Common mistakes

Writing about you instead of the reader (search "we/our" and flip); features without benefits; no CTA; burying the lead; more than one message per page; weak headline; no proof; jargon; ignoring mobile; never testing; clever over clear; wall of text; fake urgency.
