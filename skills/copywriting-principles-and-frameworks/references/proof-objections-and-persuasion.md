# Proof, objections, urgency and persuasion

## Proof hierarchy (strongest first)

1. Named customer result with a metric (ideally with photo)
2. Specific metric
3. Industry statistic from an independent source
4. Testimonial with name and role
5. Volume ("used by 2,400+ companies")
6. Logos, press mentions, awards, certifications
7. Star ratings
8. Generic praise ("trusted by hundreds") - avoid

Match proof to the prospect's industry and size. Spread proof through the page; place one near each CTA.

## Testimonial selection

Keep quotes with: a specific result, before/after context, role plus company, a quotable line. Reject "Great product!" and "Easy to use!". Testimonials need name, company and result.

Testimonial request template: reference the result the customer mentioned; ask three questions (what was it like before, what result did you get, what would you tell others); say that 2-3 sentences would be great.

## Risk reversal and guarantees

State exact terms and the outcome covered. Only offer what the business will honour.

- Money-back: "If it doesn't [outcome] within 30 days, email us and we'll refund you."
- Results-based: "If you don't see [metric] within [timeframe], we'll keep working with you for free or refund you."

Zero-risk framing: position the offer as the low-risk option; the status quo is the real risk.

## Objection handling

| Objection | Copy response |
|---|---|
| Too expensive | Cost of inaction; ROI or time-saved math; payment plan |
| Won't work for me | Case study from the same industry or role; "built for [audience]" block |
| No time to implement | Time-to-value ("set up in 10 minutes") |
| Already have one | Make switching cheap; comparison table |
| What if it doesn't work | Risk reversal plus proof |
| Need to ask my boss | Shareable business case |
| Need to think about it / later | Genuine deadline or ongoing cost of delay |
| Is it hard to set up / how is it different from X | FAQ, comparison, how-it-works |

Place objection copy in the FAQ, within testimonials, and just before the final CTA. Preempt; do not wait. Reflect the customer's exact thoughts back ("You might be thinking...").

## Urgency and scarcity

Use only when real. Preferred form is cost of delay: "Every week without [solution] costs roughly [amount]" (use real figures).

| Acceptable | Not acceptable |
|---|---|
| Real cohort deadline | Fake countdown timers |
| Stated capacity limit ("We onboard 5 clients per month") | "Only 2 left" when untrue |
| Early-bird with a real end date | Permanent "sale" |
| Grandfathered beta pricing | Hidden price increases |
| Stock/shipping cut-offs | Fabricated "early signals" or invented social proof |

## Price anchoring

Show the higher reference before your price. Patterns: value stack (cost of the alternative or sum of components), cost of the problem (what inaction costs, then your price), competitor anchor. Use real figures only. Show annual savings in currency.

## Research-backed persuasion levers (use 3-5 per piece)

If copy reads engineered or slimy, strip levers back.

| Lever | Application |
|---|---|
| Incentive and risk (always in play) | The offer itself; reverse risk with guarantee or trial terms |
| Social proof, authority | Named results, credentials, recognised endorsers |
| Anchoring, contrast | Reference price before price; before/after |
| Scarcity, urgency with stated consequence | Real only |
| Loss framing | Make the concrete cost of inaction visible. Losses weigh more than equivalent gains; do not quote a specific multiplier |
| Curiosity gap | Withhold a detail only the next step reveals |
| Reason-giving | Add a "because" to a request; any reason increases compliance |
| Commitment and consistency | Small committed next steps are easier than big ones |
| Familiarity, repetition | Repeat the core claim across touches |
| Understanding | Reflect the customer's exact thoughts, especially in objection copy |
| Disarming honesty | State one hard truth, then pitch |
| Show, don't tell | Proof beats claim |
| Quantitative specificity | Exact, real numbers |
| Instruction clarity | Spell out the next 2-3 steps after the CTA |
| Dramatise the problem | Sparingly |

By surface: landing page -> social proof, authority, specificity, show-don't-tell, curiosity. Objection handling -> understanding, disarming honesty, commitment, loss framing. Offer design -> incentive, risk reversal, scarcity, anchoring, contrast, instruction clarity. Proposal -> authority, social proof, specificity, contrast, zero-risk, show-don't-tell.

Do not use groupthink tactics such as seeding fake early signals or fabricated popularity.
