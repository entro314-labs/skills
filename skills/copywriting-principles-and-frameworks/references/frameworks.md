# Frameworks and templates

## Core set

| Framework | Steps | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| PAS | Problem, Agitate (make the cost of inaction concrete), Solve | Pain-driven offers, warm audiences, email, short ads, social, retargeting |
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | Landing pages, ads, long emails, launches |
| BAB | Before, After, Bridge | Case studies, testimonials, transformation |
| FAB | Feature, Advantage, Benefit | Product descriptions, comparisons |
| 4Ps | Promise, Picture (sensory), Proof, Push | Ads, short landing pages |
| PASTOR | Problem, Amplify, Story, Testimony/Transformation, Offer, Response | Long sales pages, webinars, VSLs |
| Star-Story-Solution | Hook figure, story, solution | Email sequences, brand narratives |
| 4U (checklist) | Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful; score 1-4 each | Headlines, subject lines, CTA elements (a house checklist, not an established method) |

Selection: cold audience + long page -> PASTOR; warm + quick action -> PAS; case study -> BAB; product launch -> AIDA; ad under 100 words -> 4Ps; email nurture -> Star-Story-Solution; retargeting -> short PAS.

## Direct-response architecture

1. Hook (headline, opening line)
2. Problem validation (the reader nods)
3. Mechanism: "Most people try X; what works is Y" (the unique how)
4. Solution
5. Social proof
6. Objection handling: "You might be thinking X; here is the truth"
7. CTA
8. Urgency (real only)

Pitfalls: starting with the company, features without benefits, claims without proof, vague CTA, urgency that is not real.

## Feature -> benefit -> "so what"

Keep asking "so what?" from the feature until you reach the buyer's outcome.

| Level | Meaning | Example direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Feature | What it is | "Automated sync" |
| 2 Function | Outcome enabled | "Data updates itself" |
| 3 Benefit | Problem solved | "No manual reconciliation" |
| 4 Impact | Business result | "Close the books days earlier" |
| 5 Identity | Meaning for their career | "The team that never misses a close" |

Cold copy opens at level 4 or 5, never 1 or 2. Bridge phrase: "which means...".

## Emotional arc (B2B)

Open with the pain (emotional), prove with data (logical), close with the relief (emotional).

## B2B outreach

Decision tree: cold first touch -> PAS, 2-3 sentences, under ~80 words total. Not cold and you have proof -> BAB. Not cold and no proof -> AIDA (educational nurture).

Cold PAS template:

```
Hi [Name],
[One-sentence problem observation tied to a real signal.]
Most [role]s in [industry] tell us that [pain] costs them [time/money/risk].
[What happens if this stays unsolved.]
We helped [similar company] [specific result] in [timeframe].
Worth 15 minutes to see if we can do the same for [Company]?
```

Rules: one CTA; no fake urgency; avoid "personalization theater" (name and company only, with no real signal). CTA ladder: see `headlines-and-ctas.md`.

BAB skeleton: "[Company] was [before: specific painful state]. After [change], they [after: specific measurable result]. The bridge: [how, one sentence]. Want the same for [Company]?"

## Micro-story (50-100 words)

Character with a problem; the common fix that failed; the different approach; the specific result. Use for hooks, case snippets, about sections.

## Classic principles worth applying

- Copy channels existing desire; intensify what the reader already wants.
- Slippery slide: each element (headline, subhead, first sentence, body) must pull the reader to the next. The first sentence's only job is to earn the second.
- Give a reason why the reader should believe and buy; test claims instead of arguing about them.
- Headlines are worth testing; category set: benefit, news, curiosity, ease of achievement.
- Future pacing: "Imagine opening your laptop on Monday and..." paints the after-state. Sell the destination, not the process.
- Pattern interrupts: a one-word paragraph, a direct question mid-flow, unexpected honesty, one bolded word.
