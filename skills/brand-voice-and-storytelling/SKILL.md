# Brand Voice and Brand Storytelling

## Core model

- Voice is the stable personality of a brand; tone is how that voice flexes by situation. Voice stays constant across channels, tone adapts.
- The customer is the hero; the brand is the guide. People buy what they understand fastest, so clarity beats cleverness.
- Every narrative has the same spine: a protagonist, a problem with stakes, a guide with a plan, proof, and one explicit next step.
- Concrete beats adjectival. Replace "passionate", "quality-focused", "innovative" with an origin fact, a process detail, or a proof point.

## Workflow: choose the job first

1. Identify which job it is: define voice, extract voice, apply voice (rewrite/review), build hero-guide messaging, tell an origin story, or shape a persuasive narrative. Ask only if the answer changes the output materially; otherwise state the assumption and proceed.
2. Identify the primary audience. When audiences conflict, pick the primary one and prioritize voice for them.
3. Gather real inputs: existing copy, customer language, founder facts, proof. Mark anything unknown as a placeholder ("clarify with founder"); never invent facts.
4. Produce the deliverable using the matching reference template.
5. Run the checks below before delivering.

## Voice rules (most important first)

1. Use 3-5 voice attributes. More becomes unmemorable. Make each specific: "like a knowledgeable friend, not a salesperson" beats "friendly".
2. Define each attribute with: a definition in brand terms, 2-3 "how it sounds" bullets, one on-brand example, one off-brand counter-example. Examples carry more weight than adjectives.
3. A voice is only usable if it passes five tests: recognizable without a byline; actionable for a human or AI writer; differentiated from competitors; authentic to the people behind it; consistent across social, email and long-form.
4. Position the brand on five 1-5 scales and record the rationale: Formal-Casual, Serious-Playful, Reserved-Enthusiastic, Technical-Simple, Respectful-Irreverent.
5. Specify tone by situation, not just by channel:

| Situation | Tone |
|---|---|
| Good news | Celebratory |
| Bad news | Empathetic, solution-focused |
| Onboarding | Warm, encouraging |
| Upsell | Value-focused, no pressure |
| Win-back | No guilt, focus on value |
| Complaint | Apologetic, action-oriented |
| Support | Calm, reassuring, patient |
| Error messages | Empathetic, solution-oriented, low energy |
| Legal | Clear, direct, neutral |
| Marketing | Enthusiastic, high energy |

6. Decide and document the mechanical choices so writers stop guessing: Oxford comma, exclamation points, emoji, headline and CTA case, contractions, first/second person, number style, product vs feature name capitalization.
7. Maintain banned-word and exclusionary-term lists with a replacement for each (e.g. leverage -> use; seamless -> easy; best-in-class -> a specific claim; whitelist -> allowlist).
8. Voice-drift check: flag any section whose sentence rhythm or vocabulary differs sharply from the rest; it usually signals a different writer or an AI-generated patch.

## Hero-and-guide messaging essentials

Seven elements, in order: Character (one specific desire) -> Problem (external, internal, philosophical; one real villain) -> Guide (empathy plus authority) -> Plan (3-4 verb-led steps plus an agreement plan that removes risk) -> Call to action (one direct, one transitional) -> Failure (stakes) -> Success (transformation).

- Two fatal mistakes: making the brand the hero, and messaging too complex to grasp quickly.
- Most brands only name the external problem; the internal problem (how it feels) drives purchase.
- One-liner: "We help [character] who struggle with [problem] to [solution] so they can [result]." Test: a listener can repeat it after one hearing.
- Diagnostic for any asset: can a newcomer see the offer in 5 seconds; is the customer the hero; is the internal problem named; are empathy and authority both present; is there a 3-step plan; is there one obvious CTA; are success and failure both shown? Any "no" is the thing to fix.
- Details, templates and the website checklist: references/hero-guide-messaging.md.

## Founder and origin stories

- Same origin and mission on every touchpoint; only length and emphasis change. Never tell a different "why we started" on About vs product pages.
- Placement drives length: About 300-800 words; hero 1-2 line headline plus 1-2 line sub plus one CTA; product-page story block 2-4 sentences below the fold; welcome message 1-3 sentences; packaging 1-2 sentences.
- The founder is the guide, not the hero. Origin supports the customer's story; it is never the centerpiece.
- Spine sentence: "We started [Brand] because [origin]. Today we [mission] for [audience] by [differentiator]."
- Details and element checklist: references/founder-story.md.

## Narrative for non-technical audiences

- Lead with the conclusion: a headline sentence that carries the essence and is understood in 10 seconds, then 3-5 key points, concrete proof per point, one explicit CTA.
- Apply a "so what?" test to every point: does it say why this audience should care?
- Pick the structure by situation: Hero's journey (major change), Problem-Solution-Benefit (proposals), Before-After-Bridge (launches, process changes), Situation-Complication-Resolution (executives, boards, investors).
- Humanize numbers: "42% churn" becomes "We lose 12 customers every week". One number per point; use comparisons.
- Translate jargon on first use, use analogies from the audience's domain, lead with business impact.
- Data narrative headline: [specific number] + [business impact] + [actionable context]. "Q4 Sales Analysis" becomes "Q4 Sales Beat Target by 23% - Here's Why".
- Structures, change/crisis recipes and report templates: references/narrative-structures.md.

## Integrity rules (apply to every deliverable)

- Never fabricate customer desires; ground them in research or observed behavior.
- Never exaggerate problems, invent suffering, or use fake urgency or fear-mongering; state proportionate consequences.
- Never claim authority not earned. Testimonials must be real; statistics verifiable and sourced. Never publish an unsourced statistic as fact.
- Never promise outcomes the plan cannot deliver; honor every agreement-plan commitment.
- CTAs must match what happens on click; do not disguise a purchase as a free action.
- State assumptions, data sources and limitations; avoid passive voice that hides agency.

## Pre-delivery checklist

- [ ] Customer is the hero; copy does not open with "We".
- [ ] One message per asset; clarity chosen over cleverness; outcome-led, not feature-led.
- [ ] Voice attributes visible in the copy; tone matches the situation.
- [ ] Filler phrases removed ("we're passionate", "quality is our priority", "crafted with love", "our mission is to provide").
- [ ] Every claim has a process detail or proof point; unknowns are marked as placeholders.
- [ ] Exactly one clear next step per asset.
- [ ] Mechanical style decisions (case, punctuation, numbers, pronouns) applied consistently.
