# Hero-and-guide messaging (seven elements)

Principle: the customer is the hero, the brand is the guide. People buy what they understand fastest. Two fatal mistakes: making the brand the hero, and messaging too complex to grasp quickly.

## The seven elements

| # | Element | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Character | One specific desire tied to survival, status, belonging or meaning. Segment by role or stage if needed. |
| 2 | Problem | Three layers: external (tangible), internal (how it feels), philosophical (why it is unfair). Name a singular, real villain. |
| 3 | Guide | Empathy ("we know what it's like...") plus authority (proof, stats, testimonials, years). Too much of either fails. Origin story is never the centerpiece. |
| 4 | Plan | Process plan: 3-4 numbered, verb-led steps. Agreement plan: risk-removing commitments (cancel anytime, no contract, guarantee). |
| 5 | Call to action | One direct CTA (action verb, button, contrasting color, repeated) plus one transitional CTA at lower commitment (guide, demo, quiz). Both visible in the header of every page. |
| 6 | Failure | Stakes. Start subtle ("most teams lose months before finding a fix"); go overt only if needed. |
| 7 | Success | Winning position/status, union/belonging, or self-realization. |

Villain: bad = "complexity"; good = "jargon designed to confuse you". A villain is a root cause the customer can be angry at, not a vague abstraction and not a competitor attack.

Templates:

- Failure: "Without [solution], you risk X, Y, and Z."
- Success: "With [solution], you'll [achievement], [feeling], and [transformation]."
- One-liner: "We help [character] who struggle with [problem] to [solution] so they can [result]." Test: a listener can repeat it after one hearing.

## Identity transformation

"From [before identity] to [after identity]" is the strongest motivator. Use it in testimonials and as a community name. Before-after identity pairs: "Overwhelmed and behind" -> "Efficient and ahead".

## Messaging-script worksheet

Fill each field in one or two plain sentences:

- Character (what they want)
- Villain
- External problem / internal problem / philosophical problem
- Empathy statement
- Authority proof
- Plan steps 1-3
- Agreements (risk reducers)
- Direct CTA / transitional CTA
- Failure (what is at stake)
- Success (what life looks like after)
- FROM / TO identity transformation

## Website copy checklist

- Headline promises a success or solves a problem.
- Subhead says what you offer.
- Direct CTA above the fold and repeated down the page.
- Stakes section, balanced (not fear-mongering).
- Plan has 3-4 steps starting with verbs.
- Success section shows the transformation.
- Empathy and authority both present.

## Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Brand as hero | Position as guide |
| Multiple messages | One message per asset |
| Clever over clear | Choose clarity |
| Feature-led | Outcome-led |
| No CTA | Ask for the action |
| No stakes | Show what failure costs |
| Starts with "We" | Start with the customer's problem |

## Diagnostic for any asset

1. Can a newcomer see the offer in 5 seconds?
2. Is the customer the hero?
3. Is the internal problem named?
4. Are empathy and authority both present?
5. Is there a 3-step plan?
6. Is there one obvious CTA?
7. Are success and failure both shown?

Any "no" is the problem to fix first.

## Ethics

- Ground the customer's desire in research or observed behavior; do not invent it.
- Do not exaggerate problems or invent suffering.
- Claim only authority you have; testimonials real, statistics verifiable.
- Honor every agreement-plan commitment.
- CTAs must match what happens on click.
