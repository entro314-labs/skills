# Voice Guide Template

Use this skeleton to produce or audit a brand voice guide. Keep the finished guide short enough that a writer (or an AI prompt) can apply it without interpretation.

## 1. Voice summary

2-3 sentences: who the brand sounds like and how it relates to the reader. Prefer a relationship image ("a knowledgeable friend, not a salesperson") over adjectives.

## 2. Voice attributes (3-5)

For each attribute:

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Definition | What the attribute means in this brand's terms |
| How it sounds | 2-3 bullets (sentence length, word choice, stance) |
| On-brand example | One real sentence that embodies it |
| Off-brand counter-example | One sentence that violates it |

Sample on-brand / off-brand pairs:

| Attribute | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Confident | "This works." | "This might work." |
| Clear | "Sign up free" | "Complete registration" |
| Helpful | "Save 2 hours" | "Advanced automation" |

## 3. Voice spectrum

Score 1-5 on each scale and write a one-line rationale per scale.

| Scale | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | Formal | Casual |
| Seriousness | Serious | Playful |
| Energy | Reserved | Enthusiastic |
| Complexity | Technical | Simple |
| Attitude | Respectful | Irreverent |

## 4. Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Enthusiastic | High |
| Support | Calm, reassuring, patient | Medium-low |
| Error messages | Empathetic, solution-oriented | Low |
| Legal | Clear, direct, neutral | Low |
| Social | Conversational, reactive | Varies |
| Newsletter | Personal, valuable | Medium |

By situation (good news, bad news, onboarding, upsell, win-back, complaint, support, legal): use the situation table in `SKILL.md`.

Channel adjustments (voice constant, tone shifts): professional networks more formal, minimal emoji, insight-led; short-post networks punchy and reactive; visual networks lifestyle-led; short-video platforms casual and entertainment-led.

## 5. Vocabulary

Three lists:

| List | Columns |
|---|---|
| Words we love | Word, why it fits |
| Industry terms | Term, when to use it, plain alternative |
| Branded terms | Term, definition, usage sentence |

Banned/avoid table (word, why, use instead):

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| synergy | Vague | collaboration |
| leverage | Jargon | use, apply |
| revolutionary | Unprovable hype | better, improved (with a specific) |
| seamless | Empty promise | smooth, easy |
| best-in-class | Unverifiable | a specific, sourced claim |

Exclusionary terms: guys -> everyone, team, folks; man-hours -> work hours; whitelist -> allowlist; master/slave -> primary/secondary.

## 6. Mechanical style decisions

Decide and record each; undecided items become inconsistency.

- Oxford comma: yes/no.
- Exclamation points: sparingly or never.
- Emoji: social only, or never.
- Headline and CTA case: Title Case or sentence case.
- Contractions: yes/no.
- Person: first person (we/I) and second person ("you", always).
- Numbers: spell out one through nine, numerals from 10; percentages as 25%; money as $10.
- Names: product names always capitalized; feature names lowercase unless trademarked.

## 7. Rhythm and formatting

Typical sentence and paragraph length, how pieces open, header style, list use, emphasis, where CTAs sit and how they are worded.

## 8. Phrase bank

3 on-brand phrases and 3 off-brand phrases, each with the reason.

## 9. Do / Don't rules

3-5 of each. Each must be checkable on a draft ("open with the reader's problem, not 'We'").

## Audit procedure for existing copy

1. Read the whole piece, then compare against the attributes and spectrum.
2. Flag sections whose rhythm or vocabulary differs sharply (drift, often a different writer).
3. Check tone against the situation table.
4. Scan for banned words, filler, and mechanical-style violations.
5. Rewrite flagged lines and give a reason per rewrite.

## Guideline document outline (full brand book)

Purpose and values; positioning one-liner; story summary; voice, tone, words to avoid and prefer; logo rules; colors (HEX, RGB, CMYK); typography; imagery. This skill covers the verbal parts.
