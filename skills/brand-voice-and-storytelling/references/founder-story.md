# Founder and Origin Story Copy

Scope: founder-led or artisan brands telling their story on their own storefront or site. Not for commodity or white-label products (lead with a value proposition) or enterprise B2B procurement (lead with credentials and case studies). For a single tagline request, deliver it plus one line on where it fits.

## Deliverables for a full request

1. Summary (3-5 points): the gap, narrative spine, primary placements, differentiators, next steps.
2. Placement map.
3. Narrative framework.
4. Copy for each placement.
5. Tone note: 2-3 sentences (e.g. warm, direct, no jargon, no hype) so later copy stays consistent.

## Placement map

| Placement | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| About page | 300-800 words | Sectioned with headings, short paragraphs, scannable |
| Homepage hero | 1-2 line headline, 1-2 line sub, one CTA | Headline roughly 6-14 words; subhead up to about 26 words |
| Product page story block | 2-4 sentences (about 40-120 words) | Below the fold or in a tab; above the fold stays product, price, benefit, add-to-cart |
| Campaign landing hero | One line plus at most one short paragraph | |
| Welcome email or SMS | 1-3 sentences | Link to About |
| Packaging insert | 1-2 sentences (up to about 30 words) | One memorable line |

## Narrative blocks (adapt order)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| Hook | Why the reader should care, one sentence |
| Origin | What happened that made the brand necessary |
| Founder role | Maker, face, curator or recipe guardian |
| Mission | What the brand stands for |
| Process | 1-3 concrete differentiators |
| For whom | The audience, without stereotypes |
| Values | 2-4, each tied to a behavior |
| Proof | One line outside About (awards, numbers, customer quote) |
| CTA | One next step |

## Arcs (choose one or blend)

- Origin: "why I started".
- Craft: "how we make it".
- Mission: "what we stand for".
- For-whom: "who it's for".

Spine sentence: "We started [Brand] because [origin]. Today we [mission] for [audience] by [differentiator]."

## Personal brand story (individual creator, expert, solo founder)

Use for a first-person "about me", bio, or origin narrative of a person rather than a storefront brand. Five steps, in order:

| Step | Purpose | Elements |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pain or event resonance | Shared experience with the reader | A relatable struggle, vivid and sensory, specific starting situation |
| 2. Key turning point | The "why change" moment | Trigger event, before/after contrast, parallel sentences, a belief stated |
| 3. Conflict | Struggle during change | External obstacles (doubt, opposition, failure), internal emotional struggle, belief reinforced |
| 4. Philosophy | Raise it to a universal truth | A counter-intuitive insight, parallel structure, one quotable line |
| 5. Results | Prove it | Effort invested, numbers and case studies, before/after, testimonials |

Intake before drafting (ask for gaps): origin (starting position, salary or situation), the exact turning-point moment, obstacles inside and out, proof points (numbers, timelines, names), core beliefs.

Style: authentic vulnerability over curated perfection; concrete numbers, dates and names over generalities; tie to universal experiences (family, fear, loss, ambition); balance story with credibility proof.

Common arcs: corporate escape (stable but unfulfilled -> health scare, burnout or family event -> leaving security amid doubt -> business on own terms); expert to entrepreneur (high achiever -> income ceiling, wanting impact and freedom -> imposter syndrome, pricing and scaling fears -> recognized authority); rebuild (rock bottom -> decision to rebuild differently -> proving worth from nothing -> stronger, helping others avoid the same).

Skeleton: hook line; before (pain); awakening (turning point); struggle; breakthrough (philosophy line); transformation (results, how it helps the reader now); closing invitation. Check every step is present; do not invent facts, mark unknown numbers as placeholders to confirm.

## Consistency

Same origin and mission on every touchpoint; only length and emphasis change. Repeat one signature line across hero, product page and packaging. Do not tell a different "why we started" on About vs product pages.

## Pitfalls

- A long founder story above the fold on hero or product pages.
- Empty adjectives ("passionate", "quality-focused") without process or proof.
- Making the founder the hero instead of the customer's outcome.

Generic phrases to flag and replace with concrete origin, process or proof: "we're passionate", "quality is our priority", "highest quality", "best quality", "premium quality", "crafted with love", "made with love", "we care about quality", "our mission is to provide".

## Validation

- About page is scannable (headings, short paragraphs).
- Hero and product page point to one next step.
- One signature line the brand can own is proposed.
- Optional A/B test: hero with vs without a founder line, measured by scroll depth, time on site, and add-to-cart from product pages.
- Brand-safe rule: no invented facts. Use "clarify with founder" or a marked placeholder for anything unknown.
