# Positioning, Messaging and Value Propositions

Order of work: customer evidence -> positioning -> value propositions -> messaging framework (pillars, proof, objections) -> channel copy. Copy written before positioning drifts. Write the positioning statement and a one-liner first, then everything else derives from them.

## Core rules (most important first)

1. **Specific beats broad.** Positioning names one kind of customer, one pain and one reason you win. "Businesses", "marketers" and "teams of all sizes" are not targets. Differentiation requires saying who you are NOT for and what you will NOT offer.
2. **The real competitor is the status quo.** What the customer does today (spreadsheet, manual process, agency, doing nothing) is competitor number one. Name real alternatives. Placeholders ("Competitor A") are not allowed in a final statement.
3. **Every differentiator passes "could a competitor say this?"** If yes, it is not a differentiator. "Easy to use", "powerful", "best", "all-in-one" are claimed by everyone. The test sentence: "Only we ____ because ____." If you cannot finish it, you have a feature list.
4. **Customer language over company language.** Internal product names, jargon and category buzzwords mean nothing to prospects. Use the words customers actually used (see voice-of-customer reference). Their description of you beats yours.
5. **Start from the struggling moment, not features.** Describe the situation and trigger event where the user is stuck, then the outcome. Features appear only as the mechanism that makes the outcome believable.
6. **No claim without proof.** A claim with no evidence damages credibility. List proof gaps explicitly in the messaging doc and write no unsupported claim. Never invent metrics: use `[X]` placeholders until real data is supplied.
7. **Benefits and outcomes over feature lists**, but name the mechanism. "We use AI" is not a mechanism; say what the mechanism is and why it works.
8. **One message per audience.** Tailor value props per segment or persona (primary buyer, economic buyer, technical evaluator). One statement per segment, 2-3 sentences each.
9. **Everyone must describe the product the same way.** Test: can everyone in the company state what you do in the same words? Define voice in writing.
10. **Positioning must differ from the old framing.** If the new statement only polishes existing copy, the "only we" claim was not redone.

## Before writing: gather context

Do not draft until these are known (ask, or infer from supplied material and state assumptions):

- Product name and what it does in 1-2 sentences.
- Current paying customers, even 1-2 (they are the evidence base).
- What customers tried before, and the status quo.
- Existing positioning artifacts (deck, site copy, one-liner) to react to.
- Top 3 real competitor names. If unknown, research them with a search for "<category> competitors alternatives <current year>"; use the real current year.

Four non-obvious questions to ask existing customers:
1. What did you try before us? (real competition)
2. What almost stopped you from buying? (hidden objections)
3. How do you describe us to others? (their words)
4. What surprised you after using us? (unexpected value is positioning material)

No customers yet (cold start): pick ONE named person with a job; describe what frustrates them on a given day; name their current hack; finish "Only we ____ because ____"; validate in 5 conversations with people matching that person and iterate until they nod before you finish explaining.

## The five positioning questions

Each answer must be specific before any statement is written.

| # | Question | Specific means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | WHO | A named role and situation, plus their current hack |
| 2 | WHAT | A concrete pain with a precipitating trigger event, plus the cost of doing nothing |
| 3 | HOW | The mechanism and why it works (not "we use AI") |
| 4 | WHY | An "only we" claim that survives "could a competitor say this?" |
| 5 | SO WHAT | A named or measurable transformation. Evidence tiers: Tier 1 number, Tier 2 named change, Tier 3 directional |

Statement template:

```
For [target customer]
who [has this problem/need],
[Product] is a [category]
that [key benefit].
Unlike [named real alternatives],
we [key differentiator].
```

## Standard deliverable bundle

Modes: `quick` = one-line statement; `standard` (default) = positioning + messaging hierarchy + ICP; `deep` = adds competitive differentiation map and messaging matrix (rebrands, go-to-market).

A standard positioning deliverable contains:
- Positioning statement.
- One-liner, 10 words or fewer.
- Elevator pitch, about 75 words (30 seconds).
- Up to 3 differentiators, each "Unlike [A], we [X]".
- Target customer profile: one paragraph with role, stage, situation, trigger.
- One-sentence "vs" statement using real names.

## Quick test before delivering

Pass all five or name the failed one and why.

| Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Specific | Names a clear customer |
| Differentiated | Competitors cannot claim it |
| Credible | Backed by evidence or track record |
| Meaningful | Addresses a pain they would pay to fix |
| Memorable | Repeatable without notes; a non-industry person understands the one-liner (dinner party test) |

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Bad | Fixed |
|---|---|---|
| Too broad | "We help businesses grow" | "We help SaaS companies reduce churn in the first 90 days" |
| Feature-focused | "AI-powered analytics dashboard" | "See which customers are about to churn before they leave" |
| Jargon soup | "Synergistic omnichannel engagement platform" | "Talk to customers wherever they are, from one inbox" |
| No differentiation | "The best project management tool" | "Project management for teams that hate project management" |
| Everything to everyone | "For businesses of all sizes in any industry" | "For B2B SaaS companies with 10-50 employees" |

Also: over-explaining the problem (prefer straightforward pattern-matching the reader recognizes in one line); adjective positioning; an ICP that is a demographic segment instead of a real person.

## Value propositions: working summary

- Jobs-to-be-done form: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]." Add emotional ("I want to feel [emotion] when...") and social ("I want to be perceived as [identity] by [audience]") jobs.
- Rank pains and gains by intensity (1-10) and frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, rare). Build the core value prop on high-intensity, high-frequency items.
- Map each top pain to a pain reliever ("Pain X -> relief: how the product removes X") and each top gain to a gain creator. Fit means relievers and creators address the top pains and gains, not every pain.
- Benefit hierarchy: Level 1 functional (what it does), Level 2 emotional (how it feels), Level 3 transformational (who the user becomes). Lead with Level 1 at awareness, emphasize Level 2 at consideration, reinforce Level 3 at decision.
- Final one-sentence value prop: at most 15 words, combining hook, proof and status signal. Derive headline, tagline (3-5 words) and elevator opener from it.
- Details and worksheets: references/value-proposition-canvas.md and references/value-prop-sharpening.md.

## Messaging doc: working summary

Build in this order and stop at a blocker:

1. Foundation: positioning recap, 3 messaging pillars (3-5 max), voice and tone with words to use and avoid.
2. Value propositions: primary (one sentence + one paragraph), supporting (claim | benefit | evidence), per persona.
3. Proof points: type, status (AVAILABLE or NEEDED), claim -> proof -> source matrix, gap plan.
4. Framework: 30-second pitch, boilerplate at 25/50/100 words, key messages, objection table, competitor response table.
5. Channel adaptation: website, email, social, sales, ads.

Blockers: no positioning -> stop and do positioning; no proof -> document gaps and make no claims; conflicting voice guidelines -> align first; unclear personas -> stop and define them.

Each pillar: headline, 2-3 supporting points, one proof point; it must address a pain or gain and carry a differentiator.

## Objection handling

Categories: price, need, urgency, trust, fit, risk. Prepare 3-5 of the most common. Each response runs Acknowledge -> Reframe in customer language -> Provide evidence -> Bridge to value. Reframe price by converting the customer's wasted time into money at their rate and comparing with your cost (use the customer's real figures, not invented ones). Prioritize proof creation by which objections come up most.

## Voice of customer: working summary

Verbatim quotes only (no paraphrase, no AI-written quotes; keep typos and slang). Record source, context and date for each. Five categories: pain points, symptoms, dream outcomes, failed solutions, buying decisions. A phrase recurring across 3+ independent sources is "gold" messaging language. Use VOC before landing page copy, campaigns, brand messaging updates, and A/B hypotheses.

## Self-critique after drafting

- Real competitor names used, not placeholders?
- Could a competitor say the differentiator? If yes, it fails.
- Is the ICP a real person or a demographic?
- Does it differ from existing copy, or merely polish it?
- Follow-up probes when weak: audience too broad -> "name one type of customer with your best results"; weak differentiation -> "what have you done that a competitor says they don't do?"; vague so-what -> "what is the most impressive outcome a customer got?"

## Iteration

At most 3 refinement passes. Each pass appends new context and never discards earlier context; label output "Pass X of 3". Validate with real customer conversations rather than more drafting once three passes are done.
