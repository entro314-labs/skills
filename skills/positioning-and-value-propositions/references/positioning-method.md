# Positioning method

## Competitive map

Columns: You | Competitor A | Competitor B | DIY / status quo. Rows:

| Row | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Best for | The customer type this option serves best |
| Approach | How it solves the problem |
| Tradeoff | What the customer gives up by choosing it |
| They win when | Honest conditions under which this option beats you |

Fill "They win when" honestly, including for yourself against alternatives. Every alternative beats you somewhere; naming it stops you chasing deals you will lose.

Sweet spot (target position):
- You clearly win for one customer type.
- Competitors cannot or will not follow.
- The tradeoff you impose is one the customer happily accepts.

## Positioning dimensions (deeper work)

- Category: existing, new, or adjacent. Choose the frame that makes your strengths obvious.
- Points of parity: 3-5 table-stakes capabilities the customer expects.
- Points of difference: 2-3 unique advantages.
- 2x2 map: pick two criteria that drive the customer's decision (not the ones where you happen to look good); place yourself against 3-5 competitors.

## Statement checklist

Statement template, the five questions (WHO, WHAT, HOW, WHY, SO WHAT), derived assets (one-liner, elevator pitch, differentiators, target profile, "vs" statement) and the five-check quality test are in `SKILL.md`. Evidence tiers for SO WHAT: Tier 1 number > Tier 2 named change > Tier 3 directional.

## Questions that surface positioning

- What are they switching FROM?
- If the product disappeared, what would best customers miss most?
- What do the happiest customers say when recommending you?
- Who is specifically NOT a fit, and why?
- Can everyone in the company describe what you do the same way?
- What did they try before you? What almost stopped them buying? What surprised them afterwards?

## Mistakes and self-critique

Common mistakes, the post-draft self-critique and follow-up probes are in `SKILL.md`. Additional pitfalls:

- Adjective positioning ("powerful", "easy-to-use") is claimed by every competitor; replace with a specific angle.
- Over-explaining the problem; plain pattern-matching works better for press and early messaging.
- Company-centric terms and internal product names; use customer language.
- Writing copy before positioning: statement and one-liner first.
- Invented metrics in examples: use `[X]` until real data exists.
