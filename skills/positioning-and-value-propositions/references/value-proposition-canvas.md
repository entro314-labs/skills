# Value Proposition Canvas, JTBD and benefit hierarchy

Two sides: Customer Profile (circle) and Value Map (square). Always build the customer profile first, then the value map. Products create value only in relation to a segment's jobs, pains and gains.

## Customer profile

**Jobs** (three types, rank by importance):
- Functional: the task to get done.
- Social: how they want to be seen.
- Emotional: how they want to feel.
Job context changes the solution (same job at a desk vs in a client meeting).

JTBD forms:
- "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]."
- "I want to feel [emotion] when [doing the job]."
- "I want to be perceived as [identity] by [audience]."

**Pains** (rank by severity): undesired outcomes, obstacles, risks, frustrations. Probe cost in time, money and effort; failed current solutions; feared social consequences; barriers to adoption.

**Gains** (rank by relevance): required (must-have), expected (basic), desired (wished-for), unexpected (delighters). Gains are not just the inverse of pains.

Rank pains and gains by intensity (1-10) and frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, rare). The core value prop targets high intensity AND high frequency.

## Value map

- Products and services: physical, intangible, digital, financial.
- Pain relievers answer "how do you relieve this pain?": produce savings; make customers feel better; fix underperforming solutions; end difficulties; eliminate risks. Focus on extreme pains. Form: "Pain: X -> Relief: how the product removes or reduces X."
- Gain creators answer "how do you create this gain?": create savings; meet or exceed expected outcomes; outperform current solutions; make jobs easier; create positive social consequences; deliver what customers dream of. Focus on essential gains. Form: "Desired gain: Y -> Creation: how the product delivers Y."

## Fit

Fit = relievers and creators match the top customer pains and gains. Rule of thumb (not a published figure): addressing roughly half or more of the top pains and gains is a good fit.

Three levels:
1. Problem-solution fit: interviews confirm the need.
2. Product-market fit: traction and retention.
3. Business-model fit: unit economics work.

Red flags: building features nobody asked for; solving low-priority pains; creating gains customers do not value; no differentiation from alternatives.

Evidence bar before concluding fit: 10+ target customers interviewed; pains and gains validated, not assumed; willingness to pay tested; early users show retention.

## Interview question bank

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Jobs | "Walk me through your typical week related to X." "What tasks take most of your time here?" "What would success look like?" |
| Pains | "What's the hardest part?" "What frustrates you about current solutions?" "What could go wrong that you worry about?" |
| Gains | "What would an ideal solution look like?" "How do you measure success?" "What would make you look good to your boss or clients?" |
| Validation | "How important is this, 1-10?" "How are you handling it now?" "What did you try that failed?" "Would you pay, and how much?" |
